# Gauge invariance raises the bulk free-boundary decay bound to any rate below six

**2026-11-25 | Fixed spatial spacing, continuous time | Research only**

For the original free-terminal quotient \(A_{\Lambda,T}(z;x)=[e^{T(L+zV)}F](x)/[e^{T(L+zV)}1](x)\) with bounded local gauge-invariant F, there is for every \(0<c<6\) a complex disc \(|z|<\varepsilon_c\) independent of T and the spatial box, on which
\[
 \sup_{x,y}|A_{\Lambda,T}(z;x)-A_{\Lambda,T}(z;y)|
 \le C_{F,c}e^{-cT}.
\]
There is an open-box holomorphic bulk limit, and the original Haar witness with mean-zero R satisfies the same exponential estimate. The neighborhood \(\varepsilon_c\) may shrink as \(c\uparrow6\). This is an **upper** bound on the weak-coupling signal, not a sharp exponent, sign assertion, or continuum claim.

The earlier [Dobrushin proof](2026-11-25-free-boundary-bulk-limit.md) supplies a coarse real-axis bound. The [local complex ratio note](2026-11-25-complex-local-vacuum-ratios.md) gives complex locality without an extensive density. Here the same product expansion is used with a gauge constraint on *each traversed time slab*. Counting only connected factors without this constraint yields rates below the one-link gap \(3/2\), not below the physical plaquette energy six.

## 1. Product factors with a weighted auxiliary parameter

Use time slabs of length Δ except the final slab in [Δ,2Δ), as in the local complex ratio construction. Each link has an independent bridge seed per slab and each free skeleton endpoint has Haar reference. Write heat factors \(1+u_e^{\mathrm{heat}}\) with \(u_e^{\mathrm{heat}}=k_\ell-1\), and spatial-plaquette path factors \(1+u_p^{\mathrm{int}}\) with \(u_p^{\mathrm{int}}=\exp(z\int\tau_p)-1\). In an auxiliary variable t, replace the two types by \(1+t u_e^{\mathrm{heat}}\) and \(1+t^4 u_p^{\mathrm{int}}\), respectively. Put a separate small source factor \(1+sF\) at the final time. At t=1 the coefficient of s in the log partition function is precisely \(A_{\Lambda,T}(z;x)\).

For Δ fixed choose \(R_t=\exp(c\Delta/4)>1\). On \(|t|\le R_t\), the bounds are \(\|tu_e^{\mathrm{heat}}\|\le C\exp[-(3/2-c/4)\Delta]\) by the SU(2) heat series, and \(\|t^4 u_p^{\mathrm{int}}\|\le e^{c\Delta}(\exp(2\Delta|z|)-1)\). Choose Δ large, then \(\varepsilon_c>0\) small, to make all these factors small enough for the bounded-degree [ratio induction](2026-11-25-volume-uniform-adjoint-leakage.md); choose the source radius small too (adding a fixed local source raises the graph degree by only a fixed constant). This is an application of [abstract polymer methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211762). Dividing by the source-free partition function gives \(1+sA(t,z;x)\); the same induction gives a constant analytic bound for A on the whole polydisc, independent of T, volume and x. Large Δ and small z may be chosen with strict margin. For times T<Δ, the old whole-path spatial ratio bound for \(|z|T\) small supplies uniform holomorphy and a constant bound; enlarge the time-independent prefactor to include that interval.

## 2. An absent plaquette costs four nonconstant heat factors

Keep arbitrary subsets of the permitted factors before forming any logarithm. Joint bridge laws on a slab transform covariantly under the same vertex gauge rotation at its two ends, because SU(2) Brownian motion is bi-invariant. After integrating the bridge seeds this equivariance holds for *every chosen subset* of plaquette factors, including the empty subset. Thus the integral of the part of the slab product *to the right* of any cut, with the gauge-invariant final source if present, is a gauge-invariant function of the link variables at that cut. This is true for a fixed subset of all factors, not just after summing subsets. An unselected heat factor is 1, which is invariant and does not change the bridge law's covariance.

Consider a slab in a given subset term with no plaquette factors selected and precisely the nonempty heat edge set S. Its transfer kernel is \(\prod_{e\in S}(k_\ell(x_e,y_e)-1)\) with all other y variables integrated against Haar. If \(|S|\le3\), S is a forest in the cubic graph. Integrating the gauge-invariant function on the right over links off S leaves a gauge-invariant function of y on S; it is constant because the vertex gauge action is transitive on link configurations on a forest. Integrating one \(k_\ell-1\) factor against Haar annihilates that constant. Therefore the entire subset expectation is zero, regardless of the factors in earlier slabs and with either presence or absence of the final source. On an empty slab this argument instead gives a constant projector, not zero. Four edges are necessary but may not be sufficient for a given nonzero term.

## 3. Turn the selection rule into an exact degree gap

Give factors independent formal variables first. For the coefficient linear in the source in the log partition function, any monomial has connected support in the overlap graph: setting all other factor variables to zero, disconnected components factor the expectation, so no monomial spanning them survives taking the log. For a coefficient of the difference \(A(t,z;x)-A(t,z;y)\) to survive, the support must meet the first slab, since all factors and reference laws are identical away from that slab. A connected support reaching the source from the first slab meets each intervening slab: graph edges share a variable in the same or adjacent slab and cannot jump over a missing slab.

The log-series coefficient is a finite sum of products of subset expectations, because the underlying partition function is multiaffine in its individual factor variables. This matters for repeated factors in the log. If the total weighted degree in one visited slab is 1, 2 or 3, it has no plaquette factor and at least one selected heat factor in at least one of the subset expectations. That expectation vanishes by the forest rule, regardless of how occurrences are distributed among the product factors. The monomial therefore vanishes. Each visited slab contributes at least four units of weighted degree. With n slabs this proves that the t-series of the difference starts at degree at least 4n, not just n.

Cauchy on the chosen t disc bounds the difference at t=1 by \(C R_t^{-4n}\). Since nΔ≥T−Δ, this is at most \(C e^{c\Delta}e^{-cT}\). For fixed T, the same bounded polydisc and connectedness of the source series imply spatial convergence: if two boxes agree out to graph distance r, coefficients of degree less than r are unchanged, and the remaining t-tail is geometry-uniform and exponentially small in r. Time-slab length and source location are the same in the two boxes. The short-time whole-path argument gives the same existence on a common smaller z disc by coefficient stabilization. Holomorphic bulk convergence follows by uniform bounds on smaller discs. Haar pairing against a local mean-zero R may subtract any fixed \(y\), giving the asserted bound for the original h.

## Controls and obstruction

A single heat edge has gap \(3/2\) but carrying only that edge across a pure slab makes the invariant endpoint expansion vanish. At γ=0 a plaquette excitation has four fundamental edges and its decay is exactly \(e^{-6T}\); the same finite-degree threshold is achieved by the pure heat contribution for a four-edge plaquette observable at both ends. The adjoint witness itself is zero at γ=0, so this is not a claim that it saturates the bound. If the final observable is not gauge invariant, the forest annihilation need not apply, and the one-link rate can occur. These controls distinguish a gauge constraint from a heat norm improvement.

No estimate is proved with rate 6 at fixed nonzero coupling; in fact the dressed bulk result has a shifted energy, and is not a substitute for the original normalization. A useful next target is to organize traversing four-edge contributions as a relative, rather than absolute, expansion for the original signal, keeping the multiple-excitation and denominator corrections explicit. The continuum mass gap remains open.

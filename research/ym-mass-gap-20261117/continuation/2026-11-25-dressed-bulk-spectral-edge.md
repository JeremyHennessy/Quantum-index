# The dressed bulk adjoint correlation at fixed weak coupling

**2026-11-25 | Research-only continuation | Spatial lattice spacing fixed; Phase 15 not activated.**

The [bulk crossover](2026-11-25-bulk-adjoint-crossover.md) took the weak-coupling limit first. Here we verify a long-time law at **fixed sufficiently small positive** γ after taking the open-box bulk limit, for a different observable: the connected ground-state-dressed correlation of R and F=τ_p. R is the spin-1 component of τ_pτ_q with p,q sharing an edge, as in the [mixed-weight proof](2026-11-25-mixed-adjoint-band-weight.md). With K=−L−γV, finite-volume vacuum Ψ and energy E, the observable is the bulk limit of
\[
 \langle R\Psi,e^{-T(K-E)}F\Psi\rangle-\langle R\rangle_\Psi\langle F\rangle_\Psi.
\]
It is denoted Cγ(T). For T→∞ at fixed γ in this interval,
\[
 C_\gamma(T)\sim A_\gamma e^{-m_\gamma T}(4\pi D_\gamma T)^{-3/2},
\quad
\begin{split}
 m_\gamma&=6-71\gamma^2/840+O(\gamma^{5/2}),\\
 D_\gamma&=\gamma^2/126+O(\gamma^{5/2}),\\
 A_\gamma&=13\gamma/1344+O(\gamma^{3/2})>0.
\end{split}
\]
This is not the Haar-normalized free-boundary quotient. It supplies neither that quotient's fixed-coupling law nor a continuum result.

## Exact finite-volume band coordinates

Use the [uniform open-box band and its rank](2026-11-25-volume-uniform-electric-plaquette-band.md), available along any interpolation with each plaquette coefficient bounded by |γ|. Write Yd_x=P_b F_xΨ for all plaquettes x, with F_x=τ_x. Put G=Y*Y, W=Y*(K−E−6)Y, c=Y*RΨ. Introduce a real C∞ compactly supported f equal to one on the common band [5.94,6.06] and zero off (23/4,25/4). The kernels of G and W are filtered matrix elements 〈F_xΨ,j(K−E)F_yΨ〉 with j(ω)=f(ω) or (ω−6)f(ω); the c entries use R on the right. Both filters are Schwartz and vanish at zero. In particular the W filter uses a bounded function of the excitation energy; a filter of K alone, or an unbounded energy insertion into a locality estimate, would not justify the following bounds. Once G is invertible, U=YG^−1/2 is an isometry onto the full band (the number of columns equals its rank) and K−E there is exactly 6+G^−1/2WG^−1/2.

## Two-support locality and fixed-order expansion

For any matrix element of either filter, retain interactions supported within the union of the two radius-r neighborhoods of the relevant observables (R uses the fixed p,q pair as its support). The error is O_m((1+r)^−m) for every m, uniformly in volume, geometry and real weak γ. For this estimate write j(K−E)BΨ as the time integral of the Heisenberg observable B against the Schwartz inverse Fourier kernel of j. Bounded finite-range interactions in the interaction picture give the local real-time approximation of B; the on-site electric terms may be unbounded ([Nachtergaele–Sims–Young](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02428)). Replacing the ground expectation of the resulting product uses spectral flow along a uniformly gapped real path deleting the distant interactions, with the gap supplied by the same [Yarotsky spectral theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0411042) as the band isolation. Explicitly, truncate the real-time integral at a small multiple of r and the dynamics to a ball of radius r/3 around B, then the product with the observable at A lies within the inner part of the union. The spectral-flow commutator tails are summable with arbitrary polynomial power in r; the union has at most two centers and anchors have cubic growth, so the constants do not depend on their separation. Polynomial support-volume factors can be absorbed by requesting more decay. This is the two-support version of the local argument in the earlier mixed-weight note.

For separated supports at distance d, choose radius proportional to d but small enough that the retained clusters do not touch, even after the finite interaction-range collar. The truncated system is a tensor product and the filtered matrix element vanishes exactly: an excitation made in one cluster cannot be removed by an observable in the other, and j(0)=0. Consequently the original entries have arbitrary inverse-power decay in d, uniformly in real weak γ. For fixed supports receding from the boundary the same truncation compares two open boxes through an identical local finite Hamiltonian, with all neglected sites in decoupled free vacua. The limits therefore exist, are translation invariant and have cubic covariance; no wrapping or periodic-volume spectral theorem is invoked.

For d≤r a union truncation has O(r³) bounded plaquette summands, independent of d. On |z|≤c₀(1+r)^−3 the truncated vacuum and band are analytic Riesz clusters separated by fixed contours. The analytic continuation of the matrix element can be written as the trace against the rank-one vacuum projector of the two bounded observable insertions and the band projection (and, for W, the first contour moment centered at E(z)+6). Thus it is bounded uniformly on this disk by the bounded perturbation resolvent estimate; no complex spectral-flow assertion is made. Cauchy's estimate gives a pointwise Taylor remainder C|γ|^{j+1}(1+r)^{3(j+1)} after degree j when |γ| is less than half the disk radius. The truncated coefficients through degree two agree with the full coefficients for r above a fixed constant: the explicit vacuum derivative and spin projections give the finite-range coefficients below. This equality matters; real-coupling proximity alone would not imply it.

Use the weighted Schur row and column norm Σ_y(1+d(x,y))²|entry_xy| (for c the analogous weighted sum) and split at d=r. The near sum costs O(r^5); the far sum is O_N(r^−N) for any N from the two-cluster estimate, with no Taylor coefficients left at such distances. In the bulk the weighted norm also bounds C² Fourier-symbol norm. Choose r proportional to |γ|^−1/30; the worst Cauchy term through degree two is |γ|³r¹⁴=O(|γ|^{5/2}), after enlarging the constant. The real locality errors can be made smaller by taking high powers. It follows that, uniformly in open boxes and in the bulk,
\[
 G=\tfrac14 I+O(|\gamma|^{1/2}),\qquad
 W=\tfrac{\gamma^2}{4}\big[(7/120)I-Q/168\big]+O(|\gamma|^{5/2}),\qquad
 c=\tfrac{13\gamma}{896}(d_p+d_q)+O(|\gamma|^{3/2}).
\]
Here Q=D+B is the applicable finite or bulk signless graph matrix. The W constant follows from the [reduced generator calculation](2026-11-25-electric-adjoint-residue.md): subtract its M from the vacuum shift N/24, and multiply by 1/4 because Y₀=U₀/2. The extensive term cancels before estimates. W₀=W′₀=0, so derivatives of Y in the orthonormal frame do not enter the displayed quadratic coefficient. For c the only linear terms are those at columns p or q, since both plaquettes must enter to create the shared-link spin-1 sector. Vacuum dressing on the R side gives +1/6, band projection gives +1/7, and dressing on the F side has zero projection against R at zero. Thus each nonzero coefficient is (1/6+1/7)||R||²=13/896. These are fixed-order projection computations, not a truncation of the final Hamiltonian.

## Bulk band edge and the Laplace integral

G is uniformly invertible by the Schur estimate, and G^±1/2 have C² bulk symbols obtained by the convergent power series near I/4. Likewise, the finite kernels, extended by their diagonal free values outside the box where needed, converge strongly to the bulk kernels using pointwise convergence, uniform tails and uniform operator bounds. The convergent power series and functional calculus give the strong convergence of the exact band evolution in these coordinates at each fixed T. The band correlation is therefore the Fourier integral of the three-by-three matrix exponential of the exact bulk band matrix. Outside the band and vacuum, the physical finite-box spectrum starts above 25/4, so the rest of the connected correlation is O(e^−25T/4), uniformly in box size. Its bulk limit exists for fixed T: represent the connected semigroup by a smooth rapidly decaying spectral function equal to e^−Tω on the positive spectrum and zero near vacuum, and use the same two-support locality argument (with constants allowed to depend on T).

The leading C² symbol after scaling 6 away and dividing by γ² is (7/120)I−(12I+A(k))/168, where A(k) is the [plaquette adjacency symbol](2026-11-25-bulk-adjoint-crossover.md). The exact scaled symbol differs by O(√γ) in C². Its leading lowest branch has its unique global minimum at k=0; it is simple there, separated from the other branches in a neighborhood, and strictly convex in a sufficiently small neighborhood by its Hessian 2I/126. Compactness excludes minima outside that neighborhood for small γ. Inversion and cubic symmetry of the actual translated open-box limit force zero gradient and a scalar Hessian at zero, so the actual branch retains the unique minimum and curvature Dγ. Its energy there and its curvature give the expansions displayed above. The mixed amplitude at zero is c(k)*G(k)^−1/2 Π(k)G(k)^1/2 d_p at k=0 (the d_p orientation vector is taken in the fiber). At leading order the projected orientation vector has entries 1/3. The two c entries therefore give 2(13γ/896)/3, with error O(γ^3/2). Ordinary Gaussian Laplace asymptotics at this unique minimum give the stated equivalent; no pointwise positivity of the signed weight at all momenta is claimed.

## Falsification and scope

The exact low-order checks are 7/120−24/168=−71/840, (4/3)/168=1/126, (1/6+1/7)(3/64)=13/896, and (2/3)(13/896)=13/1344. The earlier exact enumeration of all nine orientation-pair displacement sets controls the required bulk curvature. If the filter passed the vacuum, the disjoint-support test would leave a disconnected product rather than a decaying kernel. If the Gram normalization were omitted, already the free norm ||F_xΨ₀||²=1/4 gives the wrong energy units. Those checks address distinct failure modes. Neither a globally positive cross spectral measure nor equivalence to the normalized conditional signal follows. The fixed positive γ here is small, with spatial lattice spacing fixed. Literature priority of this specific asymptotic is not established.

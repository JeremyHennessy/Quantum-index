# Complex analytic local vacuum ratios (without extensive vacuum norms)

**2026-11-25 | Fixed spatial lattice, continuous time | Research only**

The previous [local boundary ratio](2026-11-25-local-vacuum-boundary-ratios.md) and [global overlap obstruction](2026-11-25-free-boundary-law-and-fidelity.md) show that pointwise changes on finitely many links are controllable while the full Haar-vacuum overlap is not. The following strengthens the local statement: there is a complex disc \(|z|<\varepsilon\), with radius independent of volume and time, on which these local log ratios have a holomorphic bulk limit. \(\varepsilon\) is very small and is not the real-coupling numerical bound from the comparison proof.

For fields x,y differing only on a finite set S, take the analytic branch at z=0 of
\[
 g_{\Lambda,T}(z;x,y)=\log\frac{[e^{T(L+zV)}1](y)}{[e^{T(L+zV)}1](x)}
\]
for long T. This branch converges locally uniformly in z, uniformly in the exterior fields, to a holomorphic local vacuum ratio as the spatial box and T tend to infinity. The convergence and the sensitivity to far exterior fields have exponential bounds in the corresponding graph distance, uniform on a smaller disc. For real z, this is the actual log vacuum ratio, not a ratio of stationary expectation values. Its first derivative is exactly
\[
 g'_{\infty,\infty}(0;x,y)=
 \tfrac16\sum_{p\text{ touching }S}(\tau_p(y)-\tau_p(x)).
\]

## Joint complex and space-time localization

Use the skeleton plus independent bridge-seed product reference as in the [bulk free-boundary construction](2026-11-25-free-boundary-bulk-limit.md). For sufficiently large T use full segments of a fixed length Δ, and a final segment in [Δ,2Δ). The factors are the one-link heat densities and the plaquette segment weights. Write each as \(1+u_v\) on the product reference, where v has as neighbors the overlapping factors. The degree of this factor graph is bounded by 110: each factor involves at most 12 variables, and each variable belongs to at most ten factors including itself. Taking a common graph with additional zero factors preserves the bound because both systems use the union cubic space-time geometry. The bound \(\|u_v\|_\infty \le e^{2\Delta |z|}-1\) applies to a plaquette factor; for a heat factor the bound is \(C e^{-3\Delta/2}\) by the one-link character series. Fixed initial fields affect only first-segment factors, with the same uniform bounds. Choose Δ so large, then \(\varepsilon>0\) so small, that \(2\|u_v\|_\infty\) is strictly below the constant from the [polymer ratio induction](2026-11-25-volume-uniform-adjoint-leakage.md) with degree 110. This is a standard abstract polymer mechanism; see [Kotecký–Preiss](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211762).

Introduce an auxiliary complex variable t and multiply every \(u_v\) by t. The ratio induction on \(|t|\le2\) guarantees a nonzero partition function, even for all versions with single factors removed or replaced by the other initial field's factors. Changing a factor alters its log partition function, in the analytic branch based at t=0, by the difference of two logs of ratios to the partition function with the factor omitted. Each such ratio lies in the radius-1/2 disc about 1, so these log differences are uniformly bounded in (t,z). Telescoping the at most \(C|S|\) changed factors expresses the exact initial-field log ratio at t=1 with uniform bound \(C'|S|\). For z=0 the partition functions at t=1 are both 1, so the branch is zero there: all ratios are positive along real 0≤t≤1 at z=0. No choice of an extensive global logarithm at complex z is needed.

In the Taylor series in t for a single-factor log change, every coefficient of order j depends only on factors within j graph steps of the changed factor. To verify this formally, give factors independent variables and expand the logarithm of the resulting polynomial with constant term one. A monomial whose support in the overlap graph is disconnected has coefficient zero: set other variables to zero and the expected product factors into independent components, so the logarithm is a sum. For the log change, coefficients not involving the changed factor cancel. Repeated indices are allowed in the log series but do not increase its support distance beyond the total degree. Uniform holomorphy and boundedness on \(|t|\le2\) then give an error \(C2^{-r}\) at t=1 when the compared graphs, reference product measures, and factors coincide within r steps of the source. Sum over the \(C|S|\) changed factors. Comparing different open boxes or far exterior fields changes only remote factors. To compare durations, use common initial segments of length Δ up to a bounded collar of the temporal cut, padding unused variables with independent references. Thus these comparisons are jointly exponentially Cauchy, even for complex z. Spatial factors commute with restriction of the product reference, and the final unequal length segment lies only at the remote temporal cut.

For real z a fixed-box large-time spectral decomposition identifies the limit with \(\log[\Psi(y)/\Psi(x)]\), as in the earlier ratio theorem. Uniform convergence on a smaller complex disc proves analyticity of the spatial bulk ratio there. A local bound \(C|S|\,|z|\) on a still smaller disc follows by Cauchy and zero at z=0; the constant depends on the disc radius. This is not holomorphy of a global normalized infinite-volume vacuum vector.

## Exact first derivative and interpretation

At z=0, \(\partial_z\log[e^{T(L+zV)}1](x)=\int_0^T (e^{tL}V)(x)dt=(1-e^{-6T})V(x)/6\). Subtraction cancels all plaquettes missing S. Since convergence is locally uniform holomorphic, derivatives converge, yielding the stated result. At one link, the conditional density proportional to \(\Psi^s\) therefore has from this formula its normalized first-order perturbation \(s z/6\) times the sum of touching traces (all have one-link Haar mean zero). In real weak coupling, averaging over the other local links changes this leading coefficient by O(|z|): a finite set's marginals are O(|z|) from Haar by the single-site ratio bound and sequential conditioning. Thus for each bulk plaquette \(\mu_s(\tau_p)=s\gamma/24+O(\gamma^2)\), consistently with the previous noncoincidence theorem. The \(\tfrac14\) is the Haar square of its normalized trace, and terms from a different plaquette integrate to zero by distinct edge parity.

The controls are zero coupling (all local ratios are one), disconnected components (only the component containing the change contributes), and the analytic t tail for graph cutoffs (the estimate does not use a lower bound on the global vacuum density). The remaining bottleneck is a relative long-time normalization estimate for the original signed observable; an absolute analytic cluster bound does not by itself recover its spectral-edge amplitude or the continuum mass gap.

# A positive local Gibbs representation of the Wilson fine-field posterior

**2026-11-26 UTC. Single exact blocking step from a bare Wilson input; sufficient small-coupling result. No continuum or novelty claim.**

The [preceding trace-edit identity](2026-11-26-wilson-trace-single-edit-response.md) reduced the edit response to a covariance under the fine-field posterior. Comparing the joint law directly in frame-tag coordinates is awkward: changing a fine link can change the allowed frame tags, so some joint likelihood ratios have zero denominators. One can remove that support problem exactly without removing the fallback branches.

## Exact reparameterization

Take the actual [StepData likelihood, noise and density](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/Block.lean#L26-L76). Introduce one independent uniform seed per coarse site and one per coarse bond, independent also of the bare fine Haar links. In a fixed order on the finite tag sets, use the site seed and the frame-mass cumulative distribution to select the site's frame tag, then the bond seed and its predictor-mass cumulative distribution to select that bond's tag. Use half-open intervals; zero masses simply select nothing. At fixed fine field the tags therefore have exactly the original likelihood, including the original minimization and fallback branches. Measurability follows from the original finite Borel maps and the cumulative comparisons.

Condition on any fixed coarse output V by weighting the joint product prior by the bare Wilson weight and by one noise factor per coarse bond evaluated at these deterministic predictors. The factor for e reads only its bond seed, the two endpoint site seeds and the finite fine stencil of the two frames and connector; it is positive everywhere up to irrelevant choices on null seed boundaries. Integrating out the seeds gives exactly the fine-field posterior. Comparing uniform seeds is not equivalent to comparing the original frame-tag specification; it is a different joint representation with the same fine-field marginal.

## An explicit sufficient bound

Let beta_W be the fine bare coefficient in [Wilson/Law](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Wilson/Law.lean#L20-L60), let beta_B be the coefficient supplied to this blocking step, let omega be the oscillation of G.price, and set d = |beta_B penalty| omega. The log-potential of a plaquette has oscillation at most |beta_W| omega and that of a noise factor at most d.

For two distinct coordinates i,j, let a_ij be the sum of these factor oscillations over all factors containing both i and j. Changing j in the one-coordinate conditional at i gives a positive density reweighting with max/min at most exp(2 a_ij). The total-variation bound is tanh(a_ij/2) ≤ a_ij/2 (the same bounded likelihood-ratio argument as in the preceding comparison). This compares conditionals on Haar or uniform seeds according to the coordinate. It does not differentiate the frame or predictor maps.

For a conservative volume-independent count, use R=(J+3)L+L+2, where J,L are the [pinned patch parameters](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/Patch.lean#L26-L60). All fine link bases needed for a coarse edge lie in the radius-R sup-norm cube around its embedded initial site. Indeed the site frame tests and minimization read walks inside their own patch boxes, and the connector adds a straight length-L path from a patch point and the neighboring frame box. The extra two units allow link-base and boundary conventions. Set D=4(2R+1)^4. A factor has at most D+3 coordinates, and each fine coordinate lies in at most D coarse factors, since eligible coarse anchors belong to a translate of the fine cube and have at most four directions each. Projection to the torus can only reduce the counts of distinct indices. Each seed lies in at most eight factors. A fine link belongs to at most six Wilson plaquettes, each with at most three other coordinates. Thus the row sum is bounded by
`q_* = 9 |beta_W| omega + (d/2) max(D,8)(D+2).`
No claim is made that this deliberately crude threshold is necessary.

For q_*<1, apply [single-site Dobrushin comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117) to the graph joining any coordinates sharing a factor. For bounded functions A,B supported on disjoint sets S,T of graph distance ell, conditioning on T and using the Neumann series gives
`|Cov(A,B | V)| ≤ osc(B) [sum_{i in S} delta_i(A)] q_*^ell/(1−q_*)`,
where delta_i(A) is the coordinate oscillation with the others held fixed. The bound uses that a discrepancy is confined to the fixed sites T and the path sum has no contribution of order less than ell. It is uniform in V and volume at this fixed step. Graph hops have bounded range in fine lattice units. This applies directly to two bounded genuinely local fine observables, **or** to a local trace representative and the local noise-factor edit ratio in the seed representation. In the latter case divide the covariance bound by E[r_e|V] ≥ exp(−d) to bound the change in the exact coarse trace score; no separate conditional-tag comparison is required. A trace mark already propagated through earlier blocking steps is not presumed local.

## What this verifies and what it rules out

The inverse-CDF reconstruction was checked on 256 exact finite integer-weight fixtures, with zero-probability choices and predictor distributions depending on both site choices. This checks the reparameterization algebra, not an actual Wilson simulation or a Lean replay. The result is a standard Gibbs-comparison application to the actual local kernel, not a claim of an original general theorem.

The bound succeeds for an explicit small-coupling fixed-step regime, at arbitrary coarse flag count. It **cannot** certify the required trajectory: for nonconstant SU(2) price the displayed upper bound leaves the contraction regime as the bare coupling grows. Shrinking the blocking penalty alone cannot remove the bare term. This does not prove failure of the true posterior's mixing. The next useful test must replace the worst-case bare-link estimate with one on gauge-invariant blocks or a positive integrated estimate that survives the ultraviolet limit, and must control actual separated trace insertions in the required order of limits.

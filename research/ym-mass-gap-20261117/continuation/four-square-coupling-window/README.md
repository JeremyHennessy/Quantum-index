## Uniform isolation and enclosure

To reduce interval dependency, explicitly factor I_n(beta)=(beta/2)^n P_n(beta); the free spectral ratio retains an explicit (beta/2)^(weight-10). For the latter, the outside ratios above imply the complementary free distance throughout the disk is at least .13 of its center eigenvalue. For the vacuum this follows from its largest other normalized free eigenvalue being at most .28 (all nonvacuum labels have positive weight). Then the same analytic normalization and resolvent proof as in the single-parameter certificate gives a uniform bound M=30 on the transition on |b|=.02, using alpha=(113/13)*.08/.92. The interval verifier checks that the complement is invertible and the claimed norm bound holds. It combines the exact leading formula with interval-enclosed cubic and quintic coefficients and an odd Cauchy remainder, using the same exact spin-network actions and full seven-hit envelope as before. ## Why the leading formula is more general than this interval

The symbolic binary calculation gives sqrt(2)*a^2/[16(1-a^4)] for any beta>0 for which the odd free pivot is isolated and simple in its sector. This coefficient is strictly positive: a is also the expectation of the half trace against the strictly positive Wilson one-link density, so a<1; the Bessel series gives a>0. Hence at each such beta there is an open, possibly beta-dependent, punctured interval of sufficiently small b in which the probe sees the mode.

Potential additional free-pivot coincidences are locally finite. For each fixed spin labelling q, lambda_q(beta)/a^10 is analytic for beta>0. Its leading beta-down-to-zero power is weight(q)-10. When weight is 10 and any label n>=2 occurs, its leading ratio is strictly below 1 because (n+1)!>2^n and the factor occurs with positive edge length. Hence a nonbinary label cannot be identically resonant. The same holds for binary labels away from weight 10; only the three previously described binary labels are identically resonant. On each compact temporal interval, high labels can be uniformly excluded from coincidence by the factorial Bessel-series tail for at least one edge and <=1 for the remaining normalized link eigenvalues. Thus only finitely many analytic zero sets have to be considered there, and each contributes finitely many zeros. This is a statement about potential coincidences, not identification of the exceptional set or uniformity as beta tends to infinity.

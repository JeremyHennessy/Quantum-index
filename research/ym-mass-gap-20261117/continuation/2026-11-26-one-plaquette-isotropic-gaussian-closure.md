# A Gaussian closure benchmark when the two Wilson couplings grow together

**2026-11-26 UTC. One active spatial plaquette only; exact SU(2) transfer limit for this benchmark. Not the four-dimensional isotropic lattice.**

The [nonzero adjoint return derivative](2026-11-26-connected-adjoint-return-coefficient.md) does not commute automatically with a joint large-coupling limit. Test the simplest exact case. On L²(SU(2), Haar), restrict to class functions of a single spatial holonomy. Let K_t be convolution by p_t^{*4}, where p_t(g) is the probability Haar density proportional to exp(t cos θ(g)). This is the exact four-link transfer on this subspace. Take spatial parameter s=γt with fixed γ>0 in the previous note's trace convention; thus γ=1/2 corresponds to equal spatial and temporal coefficients in the normalized trace convention. Multiplying the transfer by a constant does not affect its vacuum-normalized form. Use the equivalent transfer T_t=M_t K_t M_t where M_t=exp(γt(cos θ−1)).

## Norm convergence on the holonomy scale

Use x=√t log g with |log g|=θ, and transform the L² space into the ball |x|<π√t in R³ by multiplication by the square root of the Haar Jacobian J_t(x)=t^(−3/2)/(2π²)·[sin(|x|/√t)/(|x|/√t)]². Extend operators by zero outside the ball. For t≥1, the normalized one-link kernel has t^(−3/2)p_t bounded uniformly, and at fixed x it tends to 2π² times the N(0,I_3) density at x. To see both, substitute x in the normalization, use 1−cos θ≥2θ²/π² and apply dominated convergence; the limit of the normalized integral is positive. The one-link law in x then has a uniform Gaussian envelope.

Integrate three independent steps in the fourfold convolution and leave the last density as an integrand. For fixed rescaled log coordinates of those steps and of the target pair x,y, the rescaled logarithm of the required final group element tends to the Euclidean difference x−y minus the sum of the three steps (or the overall inverse, irrelevant to the Gaussian). This follows from the first-order product expansion at identity on any compact set of these variables. The uniform scaled density bound and the three Gaussian envelopes justify dominated convergence. The limiting rescaled fourfold density is therefore 2π² times N(0,4I_3) at x−y. Including the two square-root Jacobians cancels both the power of t and the 2π².

The spatial multipliers converge to exp(−γ|x|²/2) and have a uniform Gaussian envelope. The scaled fourfold density is uniformly bounded (its sup is at most the one-step sup), so the transformed two-variable transfer kernel is dominated by an integrable Gaussian squared in x,y. Hence it converges in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, on the full ambient space and on its radial subspace, to a positive constant times the kernel exp[−A(|x|²+|y|²)+B x·y], with A=γ/2+1/8 and B=1/4. Continuity of isolated simple eigenprojections gives convergence of the positive normalized vacuum; simplicity and a gap from the remaining eigenvalues here follow from strict positivity and compactness of the limiting Gaussian kernel. The eigenfunction equation and the uniform kernel envelope give a uniform Gaussian envelope on the finite-t vacuum as well, since the top eigenvalues are bounded below.

## The centered fundamental vector closes at leading order

Let C=√(A²−B²/4) and q=B/[2(A+C)]. Completing the square gives the Gaussian vacuum ψ(x) proportional to exp(−C|x|²). After dividing by the top eigenvalue and conjugating by ψ, the integral kernel is a Gaussian conditional with mean qx and covariance I/[2(A+C)]. Thus the centered observable |x|²−3/(4C) is an exact eigenfunction with eigenvalue q². This is the radial quadratic instance of the classical [Mehler kernel](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.18.E28).

Multiplying the finite-t centered fundamental vector by t, its observable factor is t times 2cos(|x|/√t) minus its own ground-state mean. After subtracting the constant 2 inside the centering, the factor tends to −|x|² minus its corresponding mean. The inequality t|2cos(|x|/√t)−2|≤|x|² and the vacuum envelope justify convergence of these vectors in L²; vacuum convergence on compact sets in L² suffices together with the uniform tail bound. The limiting vector is nonzero. Consequently, for the vacuum-normalized transfer, the ratio of the Rayleigh-subtracted remainder norm to the centered fundamental vector norm tends to zero. The two-step return fraction tested in the previous note also vanishes in this benchmark, despite the nonzero derivative at s=0 for each t.

**Boundary of result.** This is a real joint-coupling limit of a one-plaquette transfer, not a claim about a full array of spatial plaquettes. In a spatial lattice, the Gaussian tangent contains coupled curl modes; a local squared flux need not be an eigenvector, and the large-volume/physical-distance control is separate. Next test whether the Gaussian curl field on a finite spatial torus gives a purely kinematic return even without nonabelian interactions, to separate such mode dispersion from a genuine nonperturbative mass mechanism. No originality or continuum gap claim is made.

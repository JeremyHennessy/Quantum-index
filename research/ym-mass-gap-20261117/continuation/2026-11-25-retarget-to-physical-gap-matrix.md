# Research target audit: from the signed lattice witness to a physical gap test

**2026-11-25 | Research planning with a proved spectral criterion | No Phase 15 activation**

## What has and has not been established

The [all-time sign result](2026-11-25-all-time-bulk-sign-and-crossover.md) is a paper-level derivation for a selected Haar/free-terminal cross observable on the infinite spatial lattice, at fixed spacing, continuously evolving electric time, and sufficiently small positive ratio of the spatial interaction to the electric generator. Its proof needs the exact boundary profiles, the high-energy control, and the weighted Fourier comparison; a negative cross weight is not a positive autocorrelation. Its literature priority and external verification are unresolved. Further improvements to that witness at the same coupling would not address the first missing continuum step.

The [Jaffe–Witten target](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf#page=6) is exclusion of physical Hamiltonian spectrum near zero in a nontrivial continuum theory. The [working-group assessment](https://alexisdpc.github.io/yang-mills/references/assessment-1-2026-11-10.pdf) validates the existence-side Lean construction and explicitly does not establish the gap. In the exact [pinned specification](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/ChallengeSpec.lean), the Wilson regularity has a logarithmically growing inverse bare coupling; its general Wilson convergence is for the mixed hierarchy, while the continuation rooted in one selected character reconstructs the Wightman theory. One cannot automatically replace a selected physical phase by the mixed correlation. Nor does the time-only limit used for the electric Hamiltonian remove spatial spacing. The small magnetic/electric ratio is the strong bare-coupling side of the [Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395); its physical coefficient and spatial lattice scales must be matched separately to any continuum trajectory. The elementary catalogue of known strong-coupling [glueball existence](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(83)90609-0), [effective band calculations](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1985/desy85-007.pdf), and [numerical boundary-to-interior sensitivity](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(82)90877-2) rules out treating any of those general ideas as new here.

## A precise pass/fail target at one physical separation

Let H be the nonnegative generator in the exact target phase with orthogonal vacuum-sector projector \(P_0=1_{\{0\}}(H)\). Pick a dense span D of centered physical test vectors \(\xi_i\) in \((I-P_0)\mathcal H\), specifically smeared, reflected polynomial observables when an OS presentation is used. Do not replace \(P_0\) by only the displayed vacuum until vacuum uniqueness in the relevant sector is checked. Define finite Gram matrices \(G_{ij}=\langle\xi_i,\xi_j\rangle\) and \(C_{ij}(t_*)=\langle\xi_i,e^{-t_*H}\xi_j\rangle\) at one **fixed physical** delay \(t_*>0\).

**Equivalence.** A mass-gap lower bound m on the nonvacuum sector holds if and only if for this one delay every finite subfamily obeys the positive-semidefinite order \(C(t_*)\preceq e^{-m t_*}G\). The forward direction is spectral calculus. For the reverse, the inequality bounds \(\langle\xi,e^{-t_*H}\xi\rangle\) for every linear combination; complement density and boundedness extend it to the full sector. The spectral projection onto \(0<E<m\) must then vanish, since its nonzero vector would have expectation strictly greater than \(e^{-m t_*}\|\xi\|^2\). There is no need for an operator-domain assumption, a long-time fit, or a nonsingular Gram matrix. Positivity also gives \(0\preceq C(t_*)\preceq G\) independent of a gap. An optional common initial smoothing \(e^{-sH}\) keeps a dense set because it is bounded with dense range and preserves the vacuum complement.

**Finite-to-limit release gate.** If the reflection-positive regulated Gram matrices converge entrywise to those of the *same selected phase* at t=0 and delay \(t_*\) in physical units, it suffices to prove \(\limsup (v^*C_N(t_*)v-\kappa v^*G_Nv)\le0\) for each finite vector v with a common \(\kappa<1\). Then m is at least \(-\log\kappa/t_*\). Establish the matching of states, physical delay, centering and Gram convergence before treating this as a numerical or proof target. At the regulated level, finitely many matrices can falsify a proposed uniform constant or flag null directions but cannot certify density or a continuum gap. If an all-time route is preferred instead, **positive** diagonal autocorrelations for a total family, each with a common exponential rate and a test-dependent prefactor, also suffice by the spectral theorem. Our signed mixed wall is neither of these certificates.

An exact adverse control for a one-time test of diagonal entries alone: let \(H e_h=M e_h\) and \(H e_l=\epsilon e_l\), with \(0<\epsilon<m<M\), and test the normalized vectors \(\sqrt{1-\rho}\,e_h\pm\sqrt{\rho}\,e_l\). Choose small \(\rho>0\) so that both individual one-time ratios are below \(e^{-mt_*}\). Their difference is proportional to \(e_l\), which violates the proposed matrix inequality. This falsifies a tempting single-channel or only-diagonal finite-time inference even with exact positivity.

## A conditional way through the phase-mixture mismatch

The unweighted mixed one-time Gram inequality is not enough: small phase weight can hide a genuinely slow phase at that one time. There are two precise alternatives.

1. **Phase-localized one-time tests.** If finite Gram matrices \(G_i,C_i(t_*)\) are continuous in the character i and the phase measure has full support, then
\[
 \int w(i)\,[C_i(t_*)-\kappa G_i]\,d\mu(i)\preceq0
 \quad\text{for every continuous } w\ge0
\]
implies the same inequality in every character. Otherwise a positive quadratic form at a character stays positive on a nonempty open set, and a nonnegative continuous cutoff supported there contradicts the integrated test. If such tests are to come from the lattice, prove that the phase weights are actually available as regulated insertions with the necessary positivity and convergence; abstract continuous functions of the character are not a source of lattice control on their own.
2. **Positive all-time, phase-centered averages.** Suppose for each test there are positive spectral measures of the *phase-centered* diagonal correlations, measurable in character, and \(C_i(t)\) is their Laplace transform with the vacuum component removed. A uniform bound on \(\int C_i(t)d\mu(i)\) by a test-dependent constant times \(e^{-mt}\) for all sufficiently large t eliminates all support below m in the integrated positive measure. Indeed any mass in \([0,m-\eta]\) gives a larger lower exponential. Nonnegativity then eliminates it almost surely in i. For a countable total family, take the intersection of the full-measure sets and upgrade to the chosen phase via continuity of the corresponding one-time quadratic forms and full support as above, if that continuity is verified for the smeared data. Compactness and continuity of separated-point correlations alone are not automatically sufficient for smearing near diagonals.

The actual pinned statements to recheck are phasewise positivity, vacuum centering (subtracting one global mean can leave a static phase-variance term), smearing and the existence, or nonexistence, of the required regulated phase weights. Only the selected theory is directly identified by the pinned analytic continuation used above. Neither alternative is claimed as a property of the submission here. The mathematical target for the next pass is to locate which positive Gram test can be transported to the selected continuum hierarchy, and then confront the renormalized physical-time inequality. This is a different target from refining the already closed fixed-spacing sign theorem.

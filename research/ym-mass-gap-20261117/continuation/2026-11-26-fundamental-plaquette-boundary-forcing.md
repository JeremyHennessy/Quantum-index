# Exterior forcing of the fundamental plaquette, and its equilibrium rarity

**2026-11-26 UTC. Actual four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson measure at finite lattice spacing. Paper-level derivation and exact center-valued geometry checks. No claim of novelty or physical gap.**

Following the [single-link obstruction](2026-11-26-wilson-posterior-link-test-obstruction.md), test whether a gauge-invariant fundamental observable itself can forget every forced exterior at the smallest nontrivial block. The answer is no, even after passing to gauge orbits. The forced exterior can, however, be certified atypical in bare Wilson equilibrium. These two statements concern distinct quantifiers.

## Two explicit exteriors

Take the four edges of one plaquette in a periodic four-dimensional lattice of side at least 3. Resample precisely those four edges conditional on every other link. With the [pinned defining-representation Wilson price](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Gauge/MatrixGroup.lean#L112-L144), write p(g)=1−Re tr(g)/2. Start with all exterior links equal to +1. For the second exterior select one of the four resampled edges e and set the four opposite parallel links at offsets ±unit along the two axes perpendicular to the plaquette plane to −1. All other exterior links are unchanged.

Among the faces meeting the four resampled edges, only the central face meets more than one; each edge has five other incident faces. Every changed exterior link participates in exactly one of those twenty faces, and the four changes affect precisely four external faces of e. To check this directly, for an out-of-plane face the opposite parallel link has that face's unique nonzero perpendicular coordinate; all its connector links have perpendicular direction and are unedited. In-plane faces read none of the edits. An exact enumeration checked these conditional energies (up to an additive constant) for every center-valued block state, all six plane orientations, all four choices of e plus unedited, on torus sides 3 through 10: 3,840 cases. The geometry argument and the following inequality do not restrict the resampled edges to center values.

In the first exterior, the conditional energy is `5 sum_i p(U_i)+p(P)`, with oriented plaquette product P. It has its unique minimum at all +1, where the normalized fundamental trace W=1. In the second exterior let q_e=−1 and all other q_i=+1, and write U_i=q_i V_i. External energies, up to their additive constant, become `sum_i s_i p(V_i)` with s_e=3 and the others 5, since `4p(−U_e)+p(U_e)=2+3p(−U_e)`. The central term becomes `2−p(V_1 V_2 V_3^−1 V_4^−1)`, with the actual cyclic order understood. By the bi-invariant chord triangle inequality and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz,
`p(product) ≤ (sum_i sqrt(p(V_i)))² ≤ (sum_i 1/s_i) sum_i s_i p(V_i) = (14/15) sum_i s_i p(V_i)`.
Hence the energy exceeds its minimum by at least `(1/15) sum_i s_i p(V_i)`, and its only minimizer has V_i=1 and W=−1. This is a global inequality on SU(2)^4; a Hessian approximation or restriction to commuting links would not suffice.

As beta grows, compactness and positive Haar mass on every neighborhood of each unique minimizer give convergence to the respective minimizers. The two conditional laws of W have TV distance tending to one: the test W>0 distinguishes them. These are gauge-invariant statements because local gauge transformations conjugate the central holonomy and map each old conditional kernel to its gauge transform under Haar. For a fixed large beta the contrast persists on open exterior neighborhoods by continuity of the finite conditional expectation; they have positive mass. The same separation of signs holds under any added conditional log tilt of oscillation o(beta), by using a fixed positive energy separation between the wrong-sign region and a sufficiently small minimum neighborhood. No such tilt bound is asserted for the full pinned ultraviolet trajectory.

## One explicit equilibrium check

At the twisted exterior an exterior-only face is also forced to −1: take an edited opposite parallel link at a nonzero offset in one perpendicular direction and move by +unit along the other perpendicular direction to get its opposite bond there. That other parallel bond and the two perpendicular connectors are unedited, and all four are outside the resampled block. A small open neighborhood of the twisted exterior therefore implies p on this exterior face ≥1.

There is a simple volume-uniform rarity bound for that neighborhood in the **unconditioned bare periodic Wilson measure**. Let n be the number of sites, with 4n edges, 6n faces, and normalized Haar. For beta≥1 restrict the entire partition integral to links with chord from 1 ≤ beta^(-1/2). The chord triangle inequality gives each face price ≤8/beta, and SU(2) Haar cap volume is ≥ c beta^(-3/2) for a fixed c in (0,1]. Thus `log Z(beta) ≥ n[4log c−6log beta−48]`. The mean energy is decreasing because its derivative is minus a variance. Averaging it from beta/2 to beta and using Z(beta/2)≤1 gives
`E_beta[p(any fixed face)] ≤ [2/(6 beta)] [48+6log beta−4log c]`
by periodic lattice symmetries. Markov's inequality bounds the probability of our exterior neighborhood by the same quantity. This bound concerns one local event, not the supremum of all boundaries or the likelihood-conditioned fine posterior.

## Research interpretation and next test

This rules out a uniform loss of fundamental-plaquette information over arbitrary exteriors for this four-link resampling. It is **not** a refutation of the physical-size slab hypotheses or their averaged gauge-invariant variants. The [weak-coupling preprint, Section 14](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202611.1282) considers separated slabs, including twisted boundaries, and distinguishes pointwise from equilibrium-weighted criteria; this example instead changes four adjacent exterior links and observes one elementary plaquette. The entropy bound above is elementary and does not establish priority or novelty relative to prior free-energy and plaquette-deficit analyses.

The exact next test is equilibrium-weighted: decide if the atypical-boundary contribution remains small when weighted by the **squared actual exterior trace signal**, rather than just by ordinary Wilson probability. Bounding the latter alone gives no such conclusion. Keep the full fundamental component, since conditioning in the finite Wilson model demonstrably transmits physical, not merely gauge-coordinate, information. This does not yet determine feedback under repeated physical-scale transport.

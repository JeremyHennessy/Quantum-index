# What the fast-path limits do and do not match in 3+1D Wilson theory

This is a dimensional and spectral audit of the exact finite-skeleton results, not a construction or exclusion of continuum Yang--Mills. In a toy graph \(r\) is the number of fine edges in a marked segment; a putative physical path length is \(ra\), where \(a\) is spatial fine-lattice spacing. This is only a length identification, not a proof that the graph is a real Wilson block. In particular its finitely many active words can span entire paths, and its exclusive lengths are fixed in link count (so their physical lengths vanish if \(a\to0\)). Real Wilson Hamiltonians have a term for each elementary spatial plaquette, not a chosen few long marked words.

## Standard electric and magnetic units

The canonical starting point is the [original Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395). For explicit factors, one can use Eq. (31) of this [primary 3+1D derivation](https://journals.aps.org/prd/supplemental/10.1103/PhysRevD.108.L031504/SM_for_SU2_ETH.pdf), whose convention is
\[
 H_{\rm KS}=\frac{g_0^2}{2a}\left[
  \sum_{\ell}E_\ell^2+\frac{2}{g_0^4}
             \sum_p\operatorname{Tr}(2I-U_p-U_p^\dagger)\right].
\]
Count each spatial plaquette once as in its convention. The standard generator normalization is \(\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)=\delta^{ab}/2\), with \(E^2\) eigenvalue \(j(j+1)\). In the toy unit-quaternion metric \(-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(XY)\), the generators are instead twice the size and the positive group Laplacian eigenvalue is \(4j(j+1)\). If \(x_p=\tfrac12\operatorname{ReTr} U_p\), then the displayed trace is \(4(1-x_p)\) for SU(2). Thus in this particular convention, up to an additive constant,
\[
 c_{\rm el}=\frac{g_0^2}{8a},\qquad
 G_{\rm mag}=\frac{4}{a g_0^2},\qquad
 t_{\rm KS}=G_{\rm mag}/c_{\rm el}=\frac{32}{g_0^4},
\]
where \(-G_{\rm mag}x_p\) is the physical magnetic coefficient and the electric operator is \(c_{\rm el}\sum(-\Delta_{\rm unit})\). Other plaquette-counting and metric conventions change the constants, not \(c_{\rm el}\propto g_0^2/a\) and \(t_{\rm KS}\propto g_0^{-4}\). In our finite graphs \(t_i=g_i/c\), with \(g_i\) a physical word coefficient, **not** the Wilson bare coupling \(g_0\). A common energy subtraction changes neither a physical gap nor the time normalization \(c_{\rm el}\).

For comparison with the conventional asymptotically free trajectory, put \(\mathcal L=\log(1/(a\Lambda))\) at positive reference scale \(\Lambda\). The perturbatively matched pure SU(2) running gives \(g_0^{-2}=2b_0\mathcal L+O(\log\mathcal L)\), \(b_0=11/(24\pi^2)\). See [Lüscher--Weisz, bare lattice/MS matching and the universal coefficients](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9502001), in particular their Appendix A and perturbative matching. This is a comparison on the usual asymptotically free running, not a nonperturbative construction or proof of an isotropic Wilson-to-Hamiltonian continuum match. Its consequences for the scales here are
\[
 c_{\rm el}(a)\asymp\frac1{a\mathcal L},\qquad
 t_{\rm KS}(a)\asymp\mathcal L^2.
\]
Therefore a finite **nonzero** physical gap on such a trajectory would require the associated dimensionless gap (in units of \(c_{\rm el}\)) to be of order \(a\mathcal L\), not of order one. A bare coupling held fixed, a freely chosen energy prefactor, or a freely rescaled time are different comparisons.

## Trajectory comparisons for the actual finite-skeleton results

**Cubic scale and running.** In the distinct-tag finite graph the actual dimensionless gap at \(t_i=r^{2/3}\kappa_i\), after the scalar normalization of the spectrum, tends to the positive finite gap of a single compact slow class Hamiltonian. To match the **size** of a Wilson coefficient \(t_{\rm KS}\asymp\mathcal L^2\) to this cubic scale forces \(r\asymp\mathcal L^3\), so \(ra\to0\). Choices of fixed \(\kappa_i\) here are only numerical magnitudes for this comparison; there is no matching of operators. Multiplication of that positive dimensionless limiting gap by \(c_{\rm el}(a)\) makes it diverge as \(1/(a\mathcal L)\). Thus this particular cubic-scale gap cannot be a bounded finite physical gap at the conventional energy normalization. Keeping the toy \(c\) fixed cannot repair the comparison: fixing \(g_0^2/a\) would require \(g_0^2\asymp a\), incompatible with logarithmic running. If both fixed \(c\) and the cubic relation are imposed formally, then \(g_0^2\asymp r^{-1/3}\), \(a\asymp r^{-1/3}\), and \(ra\asymp r^{2/3}\) diverges, rather than keeping a block at fixed size.

**Fixed physical path length on logarithmic running.** For \(ra\to\ell>0\), \(r\asymp1/a\) and \(t_{\rm KS}/r^{2/3}\to0\). In fact \(t_{\rm KS}^2/r=O(a\mathcal L^4)\to0\). For the same fixed skeleton with empty leading slow potential and finitely many marked terms, this implies free dimensionless slow low levels even without assuming distinct tags. Indeed with \(T=\sum_i|t_i|=O(\mathcal L^2)\), the fast gap on the discarded Haar space is of order \(r\), the shifted fast inverse in a bounded window has norm \(O(r^{-1})\) since \(T=o(r)\), and the exact Schur subtraction is \(O(T^2/r)\) in operator norm. The bare slow class kinetic gaps (with fixed positive exclusive lengths) are positive. The same comparison works for the simple near-total theta graph: \(r\) shared links, fixed positive exclusives. Thus these fixed skeletons return to their free dimensionless gaps, not to the cubic interaction or equal-tag wells. Their physical gaps again diverge under \(c_{\rm el}(a)\). This assertion uses the exact toy Schur structure, not a bound on a growing number of Wilson plaquette terms.

**Near-total fixed-\(t\) results.** The retained-class shifts at \(g_1=0\) are shifts from \(3bc\), so their finite order-one values at fixed \(c,t\) do not leave a finite absolute retained eigenvalue as \(b\to\infty\). The full physical theta-graph gap at fixed \(t_i\) instead tends to \(3c(u+w)\), with a dimensionless positive limit and a proved smaller correction. Neither fixed-\(t\) trajectory is the growing \(t_{\rm KS}\) trajectory. If one merely changes the time unit of the fixed-\(t\) theta graph to the physical \(c_{\rm el}(a)\), its finite positive dimensionless gap diverges, and if one genuinely inputs logarithmically growing \(t_i\) at fixed \(ra\), the preceding fast bound, not the fixed-\(t\) Taylor expansion, justifies the same conclusion.

**Equal-tag gap collapse.** At the cubic graph scale and fixed \(\kappa_i\) of nonzero product, the actual upper bound in dimensionless energies is \(C r^{-1/6}\exp(-A r^{1/6})\), with fixed positive \(A,C\). If one matches the cubic scale to logarithmic running, \(r\asymp\mathcal L^3\) but \(c_{\rm el}\asymp1/(a\mathcal L)\). Multiplying the bound by \(c_{\rm el}\) yields a bound of order \(a^{-1}\mathcal L^{-3/2}\exp(-A'\sqrt{\mathcal L})\), which itself grows, not tends to zero. **No limiting physical gap conclusion** follows from that upper bound. It is not a matching tunneling asymptotic or lower bound. If instead a fixed physical length \(ra\to\ell>0\) and the cubic scaling are imposed together by force, then \(g_0^2\asymp r^{-1/3}\asymp a^{1/3}\) and \(c_{\rm el}\asymp a^{-2/3}\). This is not the logarithmic trajectory. On that forced comparison the stretched exponential beats the power \(c_{\rm el}\), so this toy physical gap collapses to zero, not to a nonzero mass. The distinct-tag positive dimensionless gap on the same forced trajectory diverges in physical energy. None of these claims excludes a different, genuinely blocked full Wilson trajectory.

## One missing transfer estimate on the real Wilson vacuum

A precise next estimate is a **discarded-subspace coercivity bound in physical units for the actual ground-state-transformed real Wilson Hamiltonian**, not for a stand-alone Euclidean spatial Gibbs law or the Haar toy fibers. As a concrete sufficient version, fix a block side \(\ell>0\), choose a gauge-equivariant retained set of block holonomies \(Z\) at \(b(a)\asymp\ell/a\) with specified nonoverlapping path geometry, in finite spatial volumes containing the blocks. For the actual finite approximating Hamiltonian write \(\mu_{a,\mathcal V}=\Omega_{a,\mathcal V}^2\,d\mathrm{Haar}\), with its normalized physical vacuum; let \(P\) be conditional expectation given \(Z\), \(Q=1-P\), and let \(\nabla_v\) be the induced vertical gradient. A useful target is to prove a positive \(\gamma_\ell\), uniform as \(a\to0\) and in compatible volumes, such that
\[
 c_{\rm el}(a)\int|\nabla_v f|^2\,d\mu_{a,\mathcal V}
       \ \ge\ \gamma_\ell\|f\|^2_{L^2(\mu_{a,\mathcal V})},
 \qquad P f=0
\]
on the closed discarded form domain (equivalently on a dense smooth physical discarded core if a core is verified). For the selected disjoint products at each finite volume, the Haar conditional projection is smooth and \(H^1\)-bounded by solving one link per product; multiplication by the smooth positive finite-volume density and its coarse marginal gives the corresponding conditional projection on weighted \(H^1\). This provides the fixed-volume domain description, not the desired uniform constants. A stronger route would be the corresponding conditional estimate on almost every retained fiber with a common constant, but that is not assumed. The exact vacuum ground transform identifies the full energy as \(c_{\rm el}(a)\int|\nabla f|^2d\mu_{a,\mathcal V}\). Thus this inequality would supply a bounded physical discarded Schur inverse below \(\gamma_\ell\) (and is not a full gap inequality because it excludes the retained subspace). In unscaled gradient units the required lower inverse-variance coefficient is \(\gamma_\ell/c_{\rm el}(a)\), of order \(a\mathcal L\) on the conventional trajectory. Establishing the discarded floor would still leave the actual retained energy/marginal and its coupling, and matching to a Euclidean time transfer if using that construction, to be controlled.

The toy estimates give neither this bound nor its negation. Their fast Haar Casimirs and finite tag-conditional multipliers arise from the deliberately chosen word set. In the real Wilson Hamiltonian the many elementary plaquettes see partial subpaths and the actual interacting vacuum weights, and there is no established uniform discarded resolvent available from those finite calculations. Proving a quantitative estimate of this kind on a declared physically sized block and honest vacuum slice, even in one controlled family of volumes or boundary conditions first, would be a distinct transfer step rather than a restatement of the finite graph results.

# Strong-coupling scope and literature comparison

The full finite-lattice electric-to-magnetic perturbative gap is not claimed as new. For weak bounded local perturbations of an independent gapped product Hamiltonian, general gap-stability results already exist; see D. A. Yarotsky, [Ground states in relatively bounded quantum perturbations of classical lattice systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0412040), Theorem 1. The anchored Fourier method here acts on the **logarithm of the actual Hamiltonian vacuum wavefunction** and gives explicit conditional curvature and fixed-product fiber bounds, including with raw spectators frozen. This is a different assertion from only a gap of the full operator. It is also different from the log-Hessian of a prescribed Euclidean Wilson Gibbs density or its Langevin generator, as in H. Shen, R. Zhu and X. Zhu, [A stochastic analysis approach to lattice Yang–Mills at strong coupling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12737), §2. The latter begins with a specified lattice Gibbs measure; the former requires proving an equation and regularity for the actual quantum vacuum. These distinctions do not establish literature priority for the specific bounds.

In the electrical units used here (t) is proportional to (g_0^{-4}) in the declared Hamiltonian normalization. Small magnetic (t) means strong bare gauge coupling. Neither the full Hamiltonian result nor the more fine-grained conditional estimates follow the physical weak-bare regime (t\to\infty). Do not extrapolate these constants or claim an Osterwalder–Schrader continuum field theory or the Jaffe–Witten mass gap.

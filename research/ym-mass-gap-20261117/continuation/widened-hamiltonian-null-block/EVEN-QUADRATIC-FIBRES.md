# The even quadratic probe sector has finite Bloch fibres

November 27, 2026 · Infinite fixed-spacing SU(2) lattice. This concerns the **quadratic coefficient on the physical free energy-15 first-order kernel**, not a physical finite-coupling mass shell.

Write $T=P_{15}VP_{15}$, $N=1_{\{0\}}(T)$, and $B_{\rm rel}$ for the bounded, vacuum-subtracted quadratic operator. Let $C$ be the link-center involution that reverses every fundamental plaquette trace, so $CTC=-T$, $CNC=N$, and $CB_{\rm rel}C=B_{\rm rel}$. On gauge-invariant binary labels, $C$ commutes with translations: shifting the edge-sign assignment multiplies each direction by a constant sign, raised to an even number of occupied links in that direction. The number is even since each finite even-degree support decomposes into closed edge cycles. Thus the center-even part of $NB_{\rm rel}N$ can be considered in each canonical translation Bloch fibre.

## All far-separated kernel vectors are center odd

The [energy-15 binary classification](BINARY-QUADRATIC-BOUND.md) leaves exactly ten spin-$1/2$ links and even degree at each occupied vertex. A disconnected support consists of exactly two components, one four-cycle and one six-cycle: every component contains at least four links; the even-degree bipartite cycles have even size; four plus six is the only split of ten. There is only one physical intertwiner at each degree-two vertex of such a component. The four-cycle is a fundamental plaquette and center odd.

The six-cycle is either a planar rectangle (center even) or a hexagon within one unit cube. Indeed a cycle using three axes with six links must use each axis once in each direction, confining every coordinate width to one. A six-cycle using two axes is a $1\times2$ rectangle. The [separated-channel replay](SEPARATED-CHANNEL.md) checks the exact energy-9 matrix: rectangles have zero first-order action; a cube has 12 even and 4 odd hexagons and its matrix has rank four from the even space to the odd. Its block off-diagonal form then places all eight zero-slope cube vectors in the even part, with no odd zero vector. An independent edge-parity enumeration gives the unweighted odd-side incidence Gram with 6 on the diagonal and 2 off the diagonal, confirming rank four when the replay's common nonzero Haar entry $1/4$ is used.

Choose the two anchors far enough apart that both the entire six-loop cube (or the fixed rectangle) and every plaquette meeting the four-loop are at distance greater than the diameter of an insertion from the opposite component. A resonant face must meet exactly two occupied edges to stay at free energy 15; it cannot act on both components here. An elementary four-loop has no such resonance; the possible six-loop transitions stay in the same fixed cube, and a rectangle has none. It follows that the subspace spanned by all sufficiently far placements, taken as whole detached first-order blocks, reduces $T$. Its kernel is exactly the separated product of the four-loop with a six-loop kernel vector. Its center sign is $(-1)(+1)=-1$.

All other energy-15 supports have uniformly bounded diameter after removing those whole far blocks. A connected ten-edge support has graph diameter at most ten. The remaining two-component supports have bounded component separation, since each detached block lies within a fixed radius of its two anchors. Therefore only finitely many of the remaining support shapes occur modulo joint translations. Each finite label has a finite-dimensional intertwiner space.

## What the canonical Bloch calculation establishes

On the complement of the far blocks, $T$ is a finite-range, finite-matrix translation operator after indexing the finitely many remaining shapes. The far blocks never supply a center-even null vector. For each $k$, define the **canonical** even null fibre by taking the even part of the pointwise kernel of the corresponding near-block matrix $T(k)$. This convention is explicit at $k=0$; it does not assume the rank of $T(k)$ is constant or claim an almost-everywhere direct integral selects a value at a single point. The even compression of $NB_{\rm rel}N$ in this canonical fibre is a finite self-adjoint matrix. To form it one may simply project $B_{\rm rel}$ first onto the finite shape space and then onto the pointwise near-kernel, since all even kernel vectors lie there.

The three-loop $b(k)$ belongs to this kernel, has squared free norm $3$, and by the [translated quadratic calculation](MOMENTUM-PROBE.md) has normalized quadratic mean $-13/336$ at each momentum, in particular $k=0$. Expanding in the finite matrix's eigenbasis shows that at least one eigenvalue $\le-13/336$ has nonzero overlap with $b(0)$. This is an individual detectable **coefficient** in the center-even canonical fibre. It could have multiplicity. No branch regularity through a possible kernel-rank jump is implied.

The separated $4+6$ interval overlaps this mean in the **full** or center-odd quadratic fibre, but is not a same-sector continuum for the even probe at quadratic order. It neither disproves an even coefficient eigenvalue nor proves a physical particle. In particular $CH(g)C=H(-g)$, not $H(g)$ at fixed nonzero $g$; higher-order mixing, identification of the rescaled interacting law, and stability under spatial refinement are separate open questions.

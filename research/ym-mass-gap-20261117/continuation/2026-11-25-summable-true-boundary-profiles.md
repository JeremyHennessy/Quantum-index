# The true band-boundary profiles have summable spatial limits

**2026-11-25 | Fixed spatial lattice, weak real coupling | Research only**

The [gauge-filtered smoothing estimate](2026-11-25-gauge-filtered-wall-smoothing.md) turns both reciprocal-vacuum and free-wall band coefficients into ordinary vacuum pairings with a controllable error. Here we take their exact spatial limits and calculate the leading signed coefficient in a genuinely summable norm. This verifies the sign of the infinite-band **edge amplitude**; passing that amplitude through the simultaneous comparison-box and large-time limits is a separate step.

Use real \(\gamma>0\) small, the two fixed probes \(F=\tau_p\) and R, and \(Y_x=P_b\tau_x\Psi\) for a plaquette x. Define the unnormalized coordinate profiles
\[
 l_x=\nu[R\,Y_x/\Psi],\qquad r_x=a^{-1}\nu[(F-a_F)Y_x],\quad
 a=\nu(\Psi),\ a_F=\nu(F\Psi)/a .
\]
Each profile has a unique spatial bulk limit, uniformly bounded in every fixed weighted \(\ell^1\) norm around the probes, on a common small real interval (constants may vary with the weight). For either probe, coefficients in different boxes agreeing within distance R of that probe and the index x differ by \(O_M((1+R)^{-M})\) for every M, provided the boxes are obtained by cubic cutoffs of common surroundings; the estimates below also give uniform inverse-power spatial tails in any box. In a bulk weighted norm with weight \((1+d)^2\),
\[
 l=-\frac{\gamma}{896}(d_p+d_q)+O(\gamma^{3/2}),\qquad
 r=\tfrac14 d_p+O(\gamma^{1/2}).
\]

## Localize the smoothed pairings instead of the bare wall vectors

For \(K=\lambda_0-H\), choose \(f\in C_c^\infty((23/4,25/4))\) equal to one on the common band [5.94,6.06]. As in the wall-smoothing note, the first coefficient is approximated by \(\langle\Psi B_s,e^{sK}P_b\tau_x\Psi\rangle\) with \(B_s=A_s^{\Psi^{-1}}(R)\), the second by the same expression with \(B_s=A_s^1(F)-a_F\). The errors in each coordinate are at most \(C N e^{-(2c-6.06)s}\), with \(c>3.03\); the bounded column norms of Y suffice to obtain this from the same proof. The factor \(e^{sK}P_b\) on this input equals \(j_s(K)\), \(j_s(\omega)=e^{s\omega}f(\omega)\). The Fourier kernel of \(j_s\) has every needed absolute time moment bounded by \(C_m e^{(25/4)s}(1+s)^{C_m}\) by integration by parts. This explicitly accounts for the logarithmically growing smoothing time; one must not reuse a fixed-filter constant unchanged.

The rooted spatial expansions proved in the wall-smoothing note give uniform exponential localization for \(B_s\) around its probe and exponential convergence of the corresponding functions when distant spatial cuts are moved. The terminal potential localization is included there. A scalar \(a_F\) can either be compared by the \(\mu_1\) boundary result or dropped from these pairings, since \(j_s(0)=0\). Approximate \(B_s\) by a bounded multiplication observable supported in a radius-R/4 neighborhood, fixing exterior fields arbitrarily; the sup error is exponentially small. Approximate the Heisenberg integral of \(\tau_x\) against the Fourier kernel of \(j_s\) by an operator supported within radius R/4 of x, using the [bounded-interaction Lieb–Robinson estimates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02428) with the electric terms in the interaction picture. Splitting time at a small multiple of R gives an operator-norm error \(C_m R^{-m}e^{(25/4)s}(1+s)^{C_m}\). Comparing ordinary ground expectations of the two localized operators uses the same uniform-gap spectral flow as the [two-support filter proof](2026-11-25-dressed-bulk-spectral-edge.md): remove interactions beyond the retained neighborhoods, with an additional collar proportional to R. Commutator tails of the spectral flow are arbitrarily inverse-power in R; support volume polynomial factors are absorbed by choosing a larger m. The result is a comparison of the approximating pairings at the same s with error no greater than \(C_m R^{-m} e^{(25/4)s}(1+s)^{C_m}\), with m arbitrary. No reciprocal wavefunction enters this comparison.

For a quantitative exhaustion take successive restrictions to doubled cubic neighborhoods of the two probes and x, intersected with the parent open box where applicable. Use s a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(\log(2+R)\) when comparing a scale R with its double and the diameter of the enclosure is \(O(R)\). Then \(N=O(R^3)\), making both wall errors \(O(R^{-M-1})\) for any prescribed M by choosing that multiple first. Choose m afterwards to absorb the polynomial loss from the filters. The comparison errors telescope over doublings, proving superpolynomial convergence and independence of the exhaustion. For a tail bound at separation d from the probes, start with an enclosure of diameter O(d) around both sets of supports, retaining the original box boundaries wherever they are closer. Apply the same argument at radius a small constant times d; the two retained neighborhoods are now disjoint. The truncated ground factors, and the \(\tau_x\)-side operator has zero ground expectation since \(j_s(0)=0\), so the paired expectation vanishes exactly. The losses at this first scale and all doubling steps are \(O_M((1+d)^{-M})\). This proves the asserted uniform tails, including indices near a boundary, using common restrictions so no artificial nearby cut is removed.

## Recover the leading terms without differentiating a real remainder

Use a single centered cube of radius \(R_\gamma=\lfloor\gamma^{-1/32}\rfloor\) and keep indices within half its radius. Exact bulk and finite coefficients differ there by \(O_M(R_\gamma^{-M})\) by the preceding comparison; the omitted far tails are arbitrarily small also in weighted \(\ell^1\). On the complex finite-box disc \(|z|\le d/[N(1+\log N)]\) all the individual l and r entries are uniformly bounded holomorphic functions. To check this, normalize the Riesz vacuum by the bilinear transpose as in the [factorized Gram note](2026-11-25-factorized-band-sign-window.md). Its norm and the band projection norm stay bounded on the Riesz disc; after a heat smoothing time \(t_s=\log(C_0N)+2\) the same estimates as in the [complex-box argument](2026-11-25-polynomial-noncancellation-window.md) give \(\|\Psi-1\|_\infty=O(|z|N(1+\log N))\). Choose d small so \(\Psi\) and \(a\) have reciprocals bounded uniformly; the a_F factor and the individual \(Y_x\) are bounded too. As before, real pairings are analytically continued bilinearly. Cauchy's bound on first-order Taylor remainders now costs at most \(C\gamma^2 N^2(1+\log N)^2\) per entry. The weighted sum over the kept indices costs at most \(C R_\gamma^5\), so this error is \(O(\gamma^2R_\gamma^{11}(1+\log R_\gamma)^2)=O(\gamma^{3/2})\). The corresponding zeroth-order bound for r is \(O(\gamma R_\gamma^8(1+\log R_\gamma))=O(\gamma^{1/2})\). Both pass to the bulk after choosing M sufficiently large. No uniform complex *bulk* spectral projection was necessary.

At zero \(l=0\), \(Y_x=\tau_x\), and \(r_x=\delta_{px}/4\). The exact linear contribution to l is \(\|R\|^2(1/7-1/6)=-1/896\) at each of p,q: the first part is the band projector between electric energies six and thirteen, the second is the reciprocal vacuum derivative. The term from differentiating the source vacuum lies in the free plaquette band after projection and has zero Haar pairing with R. Other indices are zero by the same shared-edge support check as the finite-box residue. This proves the expansions.

## Consequence for the candidate infinite-band edge

With the exact bulk Gram operator from the [dressed band result](2026-11-25-dressed-bulk-spectral-edge.md), the orthonormal profile coordinates are \(G^{-1/2}l\), \(G^{-1/2}r\) (real). In Fourier variables their uniform symbols near zero are obtained from the summable profiles, and \(G=I/4+O(\sqrt\gamma)\) in the weighted norm. The lowest orientation projector at zero differs from the all-1/3 matrix by \(O(\sqrt\gamma)\) by the exact band symbol estimates. Thus their signed amplitude at the isolated bulk band minimum is
\[
 -\gamma/1344+O(\gamma^{3/2})<0
\]
for small enough γ. This is no longer just a fixed-volume linear coefficient; it is a signed statement about the candidate translation-invariant band pairing. What remains is to prove that the band-only expression in the growing boxes from the original signal can be replaced by that infinite-volume pairing with an error little-o of \(e^{-m_\gamma T}T^{-3/2}\). Spatial locality at a fixed time or summability of profiles alone does not justify that interchange. No eventual sign of the actual quotient or continuum mass gap is asserted here.

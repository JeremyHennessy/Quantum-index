# Local edit stability of the represented Wilson exponent and forced cover sum

**2026-11-26 UTC. Fixed blocking level; uniform in fine cutoff and period. This is a configuration comparison, not a screening or decay theorem.**

The [finite-energy comparison](2026-11-26-blocked-trace-finite-energy.md) controls the full coarse density w under changes of k coarse links by exp(±d k). Here the same comparison is proved for the represented forced covering sum G itself. In particular we need not compare the entire torus with a flat configuration when estimating how G changes.

Write w=exp(X)G as in [the pinned history](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/History.lean#L151-L200). At a fixed blocking level, the inputs controlling the bounds below are fixed and the actual inverse coupling stays in the fixed coupling band. We keep the same history, representation, level, fine cutoff, and period in comparing two configurations. It suffices to edit one bond and telescope. The constant vacuum term in X cancels.

* **Clipped direct plaquette terms.** The edit changes at most six unoriented plaquette locations in four dimensions. The chord lies in [0,2] and the clipped direct term is uniformly bounded.
* **Transport square with the kept mask.** In [PathActions.lean](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/PathActions.lean#L156-L216), the ostensibly global kept test can flip only if a changed plaquette lies within R=ceil(relaxation × log(2/threshold)) of the anchor, because beyond that radius its test is automatically satisfied. Count the anchors in this fixed-radius union with the six orientations, bounding their full contribution using the uniform bound on the row norm. For the change in the row itself, each walk-plus-plaquette slot of length l can meet the edited link at no more than O(l+1) translated anchors. Each such change is bounded by four times the absolute row coefficient, since the transported curvature norm is at most two. The absolute first length moment is uniformly bounded by the exponential row budget. The square has bounded Lipschitz constant on the bounded range of row norms. This also works for winding walks: count potential translated coincidences on the cover, an upper bound after projection to the torus.
* **Canonical atoms of orders 2, 3 and 4.** Their input reads paths of total length l and their health masks read flags on the footprint. By the [flag definitions](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/Masks.lean#L26-L98), only sites on edited plaquettes can change flags. There are at most O(l+1) translated affected anchors per atom, including the one-plaquette flag halo. The change per anchor is at most twice the atom's supremum bound 2^order × magnitude. The first length moment is again controlled by the fixed positive-width exponential budget. The average over signed orientations preserves the bounds as it sends a single edited link to a single edited link.
* **Masked regular terms.** Let x be the charged anchor of a support P. The anchor is in P. A changed term must have P within one lattice unit of an endpoint of the edited link (either its own read or a changed incident flag). In the [connected cover](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Lattice/Support.lean#L39-L69) by q balls of radius B, all points of P are at distance at most (2B+1)q from x: the graph joining touching or adjacent cover balls is connected, and consecutive centers are at distance at most 2B+1. Consequently any changed term at x has q≥(dist(x,edit)-O(1))/(2B+1), where the distance to the edited link is the minimum distance to its endpoints. Its bare supremum bound is its charged mass times exp(-supportExponent × q). The [per-anchor cap](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/Activities.lean#L88-L100), followed by summation of this exponential over x, bounds the total change uniformly in the period. The number of sites in a torus ball of radius s is at most (2s+1)^4, including for small tori. This argument does not require the auxiliary bound assigned to an address to be translation invariant.

This proves |X(V)-X(V')|≤C_m k for configurations differing on k links, with a finite fixed-stage C_m independent of cutoff and period. Positivity of both coarse densities and the representation now give
\[
 e^{-(C_m+d)k}\ \le\ G(V)/G(V')\ \le\ e^{(C_m+d)k}.
\]
For example a genuinely bounded repair that deletes a root component, preserves all exterior covering factors, and introduces no new component would yield a lower bound on that root's G_C. The two-sided comparison alone does not supply that repair: a change in flags can enable or disable a large eligible support, and overlap at even one vertex changes the partition of addresses. Nor does the comparison bound the size distribution of the affected root or the two-insertion conditional covariance.

The new result is stability of the *positive total* covering sum under finitely many bond edits. It is not positivity of individual activities or of individual component factors. It does not improve the fixed-period trace bound to a volume-uniform one until the repair and its tails are controlled.

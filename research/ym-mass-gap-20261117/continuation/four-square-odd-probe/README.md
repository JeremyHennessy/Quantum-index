# Four-square reflection-odd mode: every one-face probe starts at order at least four

2026-11-27, fixed-spacing SU(2) Wilson open four-square strip, same conventions as [the exact band certificate](../four-square-perimeter-band/README.md). This is a local cancellation result, not proof that the asymmetric probe is exactly dark, and not a mass-gap or novelty claim.

At spatial coupling b=0 let o be (124-134)/sqrt(2) in the binary loop notation and v be the vacuum. The bases and spin selection rules are from the [physical spin-network construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9906036). Let T(b)=M K M and M=exp(bV/2), where V is the sum of four half traces. Use analytic vectors psi_o=P_o(b)o and psi_v=P_v(b)v, where P_o acts in the odd reflection sector. They agree with o,v at zero, and are nonzero in a neighborhood of zero. The transition tested is the bilinear analytic function f_i(b)=psi_o^T W_i psi_v (transpose in the real basis). For real b this differs from the physically unit-normalized matrix element only by the two vector norms.

## Why second order cancels
Set u=M psi; the generalized eigenproblem is K u=lambda(b) exp(-bV)u, and the transition is u_o^T W_i exp(-bV)u_v. Eigenvalue first derivatives vanish by the simultaneous center flip of all face holonomies (flip each remaining top link on the tree gauge). Thus away from the free eigenspace the eigenvector coefficients are u_1=-lambda(K-lambda)^(-1)V u_0 and u_2=lambda(K-lambda)^(-1)(V^2 u_0/2-V u_1). The term lambda_2 u_0 cannot contribute off the band, and all second-order normalization terms multiply a zero direct transition. At first order a component along the original free vector is absent by center symmetry; in the odd reflection sector no other vector shares its free eigenvalue.

Link parity forces any degree-two term from the vacuum to 124 or 134 to include the two other distinct faces of its target (if the probe is in that target); otherwise the term vanishes. Each link is hit at most twice. Starting from the vacuum, a link hit exactly once has n=1, and a link hit twice must finish at n=0, so every intermediate link label in these paths is binary. If flipping a face connects k neighboring runs (k=0,1,2), its matrix element is 2^(-1-k): the half trace contributes 1/2, and each neighboring independent Haar loop product gives 1/2 by fundamental character contraction. This rule holds in reverse by self-adjointness. Hence an exact coefficient can be computed with 16 binary masks. The only potentially resonant free odd denominator would be another perimeter-scale vector at an intermediate step; none occurs on the contributing paths (verified directly by exact symbolic denominators). The included script assembles all divisions of two spatial insertions between the two vectors and the exponential between them, and verifies zero separately for BOTH target components and EACH of the four probes. Entries with absent probe are structural zeros; the others cancel exactly.

All odd degrees vanish: the center flip interchanges b and -b, sends every W_i to -W_i and sends the zero-coupling vector o to -o while fixing v. Thus f_i is even. Together with the zero constant and second-order coefficients, this gives f_i=O(b^4). Reflection still forces f_1=-f_4 and f_2=-f_3. No conclusion about a nonzero fourth coefficient follows.

## A convergent bound
For each of the vacuum and odd pivots lambda, use the spectral circle radius 0.1 lambda in its reflection sector. At the odd pivot the previous exterior gap implies distance at least 0.1 lambda to the sector spectrum; the vacuum gap follows from a_1<1/4 and every nonvacuum label having positive weight. For complex |b|<=R=0.01, ||V||<=4, ||exp(-bV)-I|| <= rho/(1-rho), rho=4R. Factoring T-z=M(K-z exp(-bV))M, Neumann inversion on the circle yields a Riesz projector norm bounded by (1-rho)^(-1)/(1-11 rho/(1-rho)) <2. Consequently |f_i|<=4 on that disk and, using evenness, |f_i(b)|<=4(|b|/R)^4/(1-(|b|/R)^2) for |b|<R. At b=0.001 this is <0.000405, for the stated analytic normalization, not a claim about a measured signal. The first possible signal is fourth order, whose selection paths can access higher spin and cannot be computed by reusing the binary shortcut.

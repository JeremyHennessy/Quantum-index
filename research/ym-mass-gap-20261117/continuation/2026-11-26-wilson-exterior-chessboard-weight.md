# An equilibrium-weighted bound for forced high-curvature Wilson exteriors

**2026-11-26 UTC. Known Wilson reflection positivity and chessboard estimates applied to the explicit exterior. Not a physical correlation decay result.**

The [fundamental-plaquette exterior example](2026-11-26-fundamental-plaquette-boundary-forcing.md) shows two arbitrary exteriors can make a local Wilson loop give opposite answers at large beta. A bare probability bound of order log(beta)/beta did not settle its contribution to a squared signal. For the identified bad-curvature event one can get an exponential estimate on periodic tori with dyadic tile counts, and hence bound its contribution for polynomially scaled *bare* sources.

## Reflection and dissemination

Use the actual bare SU(2) measure exp(−beta sum_p p(U_p)), p(g)=1−Re tr(g)/2. On an even torus, use coordinate reflections through bonds and observables of gauge-invariant closed loops contained in one half. Wilson reflection positivity applies. A direct check for this algebra: crossing links at both reflecting cuts form a matching when the side is at least four. At fixed crossing values, perform gauge changes at the boundary vertices of just one half to set all crossing links to 1; the within-half gauge-invariant observables and Haar integration are unchanged. The remaining crossing factors then couple each tangential link with its reflected counterpart by exp(beta Re tr(uv^−1)/2). That kernel is positive definite since the normalized fundamental character is positive definite and products and positive sums preserve this property. The two remaining within-half actions reflect into each other. This verifies the needed positive inner product for bounded gauge-invariant functions, including indicators by approximation. It does not assert positivity of a coarse likelihood conditioned on an arbitrary V.

Tile the torus by cubes of b^4 sites, b>=2, with a dyadic number of tiles per axis (at least two). An event B supported on loops in one tile can be reflected from tile to tile. We use the standard [chessboard estimate](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/mp_arc/mp_arc/c/05/05-434.pdf#page=18): its probability is at most the probability of its full dissemination raised to the reciprocal number of tiles. The use of plaquette class functions is consistent with the [Wilson multireflection calculation in Faria da Veiga and O’Carroll, Theorem 4 proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.07376#S6.SS5), although their source and partition estimates are different. Reflection reverses the plaquette product at most up to conjugacy and inverse, so p is unchanged. A loop within a tile remains internal to its reflected tile. For one face per tile, the reflected positions were checked explicitly on sides 4, 8 and 16 for admissible b=2,4 (4,640 tile placements).

If B requires r specified distinct fully internal plaquettes to have price ≥ t>0, then the disseminated event near all tiles forces total energy ≥ r t times the number of tiles, with no duplicated faces across disjoint tiles. Its unnormalized integral is at most exp(−beta r t times number of tiles). Divide by the elementary partition lower bound in the preceding note, log Z≥n(4log c−6log beta−48), and take the chessboard root. For beta≥1 this proves
`P_beta(B) ≤ min(1, exp[−r t beta + b^4(48 + 6 log beta − 4 log c)])`.
Here c is a fixed SU(2) Haar cap lower-bound constant. In particular the exterior from the preceding note has a **fully exterior** face with p≥1 in the bad neighborhood. Translation allows that single face to sit inside a b=2 tile; no constraint on the gauge representatives is needed. Hence its probability is bounded by a fixed polynomial in beta times exp(−beta), uniformly over the indicated torus sizes. This comparison makes no statement about arbitrarily chosen exteriors, odd tori, or the coarse flagged law.

## What survives signal weighting

Let F be any bounded fine-lattice source and let S=E[F−E F | exterior sigma field]. If C is an exterior-measurable event contained in B, conditional Jensen gives
`E[1_C |S|²] ≤ E[1_C |F−E F|²] ≤ 4 ||F||_infinity² P(B) directly.`
Thus for a fixed finite-stencil bare plaquette polynomial with coefficients growing at most polynomially in beta, its **absolute** squared exterior signal on this event is exponentially small in beta (for a fixed tile and pattern). This is not a bound on the fraction of an already tiny squared signal. It also does not establish a result for the matched continuum trace: if the deterministic normalization includes a factor a^(−4), a squared estimate naively pays a^(−8). When beta/log(1/a)→kappa>0, this argument only certifies vanishing at that physical scale if r t kappa>8, subject to other factors being polynomial. A single p≥1 event cannot be assumed to meet that inequality. For a conditioned fine-field law, the bare reflection positivity argument cannot simply be reused, as the coarse likelihood depends on V.

# The fourth-order Wilson band is local in a dressed plaquette basis

**2026-11-27 UTC. Fixed temporal coupling t>0; finite even spatial tori.** This gives the fourth-order locality estimate asked for in the [momentum calculation](2026-11-27-wilson-momentum-even-expansion.md), not a uniform estimate on the remaining series. [Münster §§2–3](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1985/desy85-007.pdf) already used projected dressed plaquette sources and computed eight nontrivial isotropic strong-coupling orders. [Hörmann–Schmidt §§2.4 and 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04774) explain the general difficulty of cluster additivity for undressed projective transformations. Here the temporal coupling is held fixed and only spatial couplings are Taylor expanded. No novelty priority claim.

## The basis and its independent-coupling test

Let T(v)=exp(Σ_b v_b W_b/2) K exp(Σ_b v_b W_b/2) on the physical spatial Hilbert space. Near zero, write Ω for the isolated unit vacuum (positive overlap with the constant), λ₀ for its eigenvalue, and Π for the isolated q-band projector. Form columns X_p=Π f_p Ω for f_p=Tr U_p. Set J=X(X*X)^(−1/2) and H=log(J*TJ/λ₀). These matrices are real analytic near zero on each fixed torus and J identifies the full q-band; subtracting the mean of f_p would not change X_p. The unperturbed basis and Hessian agree with the earlier convention: an analytic change of basis that is the identity at zero cannot change the second coefficient of a matrix whose constant term is scalar and whose first coefficient vanishes. At higher order the choice of basis matters. Edge-center flips act on couplings and on source columns by their plaquette boundary signs. In particular the all-plaquette flip on an even torus makes H even under simultaneous negation.

To test a monomial for matrix entry (p,b), set all couplings to zero outside its insertion faces, and partition the edge sets of its insertion and endpoint faces by edge-sharing. Treat leftover edges as separate factors. On ambient link space K and the tilted transfer factor over these components. Each factor is invariant under its own vertex gauge group, even when a different factor shares a vertex but not an edge. Restrict first to the tensor product of component singlets. The component vacuum product is the physical vacuum near zero by the isolated simple eigenvalue. In this sector the q-band is the direct sum of a single component q-band tensored with the exact spectator vacua: at zero a component physical excitation has eigenvalue at most q, two have at most q²<q, and the isolated q cluster persists near zero. The component physical bound follows from the same spin-network support argument as in the full spatial graph. A projected source contained in one component lies entirely in that summand.

**Crossing-source check.** The sector projector onto the separate component singlets commutes with T on ambient space and preserves the diagonal, physical gauge-singlet space. Hence it commutes with the physical spectral projector Π; no claim that individual component gauge transformations preserve the physical subspace is needed. A source f_c whose face crosses components has a vertex where consecutive edges lie in different components. At that vertex the center element of just one of those component gauge groups negates f_c, since it multiplies exactly one incident source edge by −1. The exact vacuum is a component singlet. Consequently the separate-singlet projector kills f_c Ω and also kills Π f_c Ω. This works even for inactive crossing faces among the *other columns* in the full Gram matrix. Their Gram entries with all component-singlet columns are zero. The latter columns have mutually orthogonal component blocks. Therefore the Gram inverse square root and the restricted normalized transfer preserve these blocks. The spectator vacuum factors cancel exactly upon dividing by λ₀. The log also preserves the blocks; a disconnected insertion cannot change an entry localized in another component, and an entry between two distinct components vanishes. Thus every contributing monomial has edge-connected union of its insertions and endpoint faces. Only a local identity at fixed Taylor order is asserted; the open analytic neighborhood may still shrink with volume.

## Fourth-order range and a conditional path to summing the series

At fourth order, there are six face occurrences including the endpoints. Independent center flips demand even incidence on each edge. The faces of odd multiplicity therefore form a mod-2 cubical 2-cycle. A connected set of at most six faces lifts without wrap on a torus with all sides at least 14. A finite cubical cycle in the cubic lattice bounds a finite cubical 3-chain. If the filling uses at least two cubes, its three projections have at least five occupied columns in total: any two distinct cubes share at most one of the three projected columns. Each occupied column forces at least two exposed faces normal to that axis, so the boundary has at least ten faces. Hence the odd subset must be empty or the six faces of one cube. With the odd subset empty, there are at most three distinct faces and their union is connected. In either case the endpoints are at distance at most two in the edge-sharing graph; for a cube only opposite faces need the second step.

Thus the fourth Taylor coefficient H₄ has displacement norm at most two in cell-center lattice units. At fixed t, there are finitely many anchored connected patterns; for each pattern its coefficient depends only on its bounded component by the exact factorization above. Lattice inversion gives paired positive and negative displacements at each orientation entry (SU(2) trace is unchanged by reverse orientation). For the cell-centered Fourier symbol, use 1−cos(k·x) ≤ |k|²|x|²/2 on each pair. Then ||H₄(k)−H₄(0)|| ≤ C₄(t)|k|², with a finite volume-independent constant. This is a matrix difference; it does not on its own compare exact perturbed eigenvalues with the quadratic leading dispersion.

More generally a degree-n connected coefficient has edge-distance at most n+1 on tori large enough to avoid its wraps, before imposing parity. The anchored shapes and multiplicity choices grow at most exponentially in n: encode each connected shape by a depth-first walk of at most 2(n+1) steps on the degree-12 face graph and count weak compositions of n over at most n+2 faces. An exponential bound on the absolute coefficient of each anchored monomial, uniform in volume at fixed t, would thus make the second-moment displacement sum converge at small |v| and give the desired O(v⁴|k|²) momentum-matrix difference. Such a bound is what still needs proof; locality at each fixed order does not provide it. A Cauchy estimate from treating the whole cluster perturbation as a sum of O(n) terms with only a fixed spectral separation can cost n^n and does not close this argument.

**Independent geometry check.** Starting from one face and recursively adding any unused face incident to a deterministic uncovered edge, searched 353 distinct subsets of size at most six. The only closed nonempty outcomes were the two incident unit cubes. All 30 within-cube face pairs satisfied the two-step range. This corroborates the parity geometry, not the analytic convergence. No uniform summation, weak-coupling continuum matching, new Lean replay, or mass-gap proof.

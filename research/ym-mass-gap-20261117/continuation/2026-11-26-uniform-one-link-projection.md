# A coupling-uniform linear projection bound on a Wilson link

**2026-11-26 UTC. Exact conditional SU(2) bound. Not a Wilson shell contraction.**

This complements the [one-link Poisson expansion](2026-11-26-wilson-langevin-trial-and-one-link-control.md), which constructs higher polynomial trials with fixed-order large-staple control. Here a linear trial suffices for a uniform result on *linear conditional responses*, including small staples.

Let x be the signed coordinate along the staple direction, and \(y_1,y_2,y_3\) perpendicular coordinates on the unit S³. At zero staple choose any orthonormal basis. The conditional probability is proportional to \(e^{t x}\) times Haar with t≥0. Write \(\mathcal L\) for the same round reversible generator. By axial symmetry its linear generator images \(\mathcal Lx,\mathcal Ly_i\) are orthogonal, with \(\mathcal Lx=t(1-x^2)-3x\) and \(\mathcal Ly_i=-(3+tx)y_i\). Project in conditional L² onto their span. The proportion of variance captured from any centered linear response is **at least \(3/10\)**, uniformly in t. The proof that follows uses no numerical inequalities.

## Two elementary bounds for the axial law

Let \(m_d(t)\) be the mean of x under the axial law proportional to \(e^{tx}(1-x^2)^{(d-3)/2}\,dx\) for d=4 or 6. Integration by parts and differentiation give \(m_d'=1-(d-1)m_d/t-m_d^2\). The subsolution \(f=t/(d+t)\) has strictly smaller derivative than this vector field at f for t>0, the difference being \((d+1)t/(d+t)^2\). Near zero \(m_d=t/d+O(t^3)>f\); no first crossing is possible. Thus \(m_d\ge t/(d+t)\). Also \(m_4'\le m_4/t\): by integration by parts \(m_4/t=E[1-x^2]/3\), which decreases since the density of \(|x|\) contains the factor \(\cosh(t|x|)\), whose ratio at larger to smaller t increases with \(|x|\).

A second variance bound follows by writing \(z=t(1-x)\). Its density is proportional to \(e^{-z}\sqrt z\sqrt{2-z/t}\) on [0,2t]. It is stochastically dominated by \(\Gamma(3/2,1)\) because the density ratio (extended by zero) is decreasing. For larger t its density ratio to the smaller-t law is increasing on the common support, with any extra mass above the old support. Therefore \(Ez\) is nondecreasing in t and at most 3/2. Differentiating \(Ez=t(1-m_4)\) gives \(t^2m_4'\le Ez\le3/2\).

## Radial and transverse projections

Put \(a=E[1-x^2]=3m_4/t\) and \(v=m_4'\). By reversibility \(\langle x-m_4,\mathcal Lx\rangle=-a\) and \(\|\mathcal Lx\|_2^2=a(3+2tm_6)\). The latter uses the derivative of \(\mathcal Lx\) in the scalar Dirichlet form and the identity that weighting by \(1-x^2\) changes d=4 to d=6. The captured radial fraction is \(a/[v(3+2tm_6)]\). For \(0<t\le7/2\) it is at least \(3/(3+2t)\ge3/10\), using \(m_4'\le m_4/t\). For \(t\ge7/2\) the lower bound is \(2t^2/((t+4)(3+2t))\), increasing and at least \(49/150>3/10\). At t=0 the fraction is 1.

For a transverse coordinate y, weighting by \(y^2\) likewise gives axial law d=6 since \(E[y^2\mid x]=(1-x^2)/3\). The captured transverse fraction is \(E_6[3+tx]^2/E_6[(3+tx)^2]\), where the numerator means the square of the expectation. It is at least \((3+tm_6)^2/(3+t)^2\ge9/25\). Indeed \(3+tm_6\ge(t^2+3t+18)/(t+6)\ge3(3+t)/5\), the final polynomial difference being \(2t^2-12t+36>0\). Orthogonality across the four coordinates extends the weaker fraction 3/10 to every linear combination. No normalization of a physical time step is involved. For numerical checks only, using 200,000 midpoint subintervals in z on [0,min(2t,72)], at t=1,3.5,32,10000 the radial fractions are approximately .97693, .93394, .99872, .999999 and transverse fractions .98457, .95085, .99776, 1.00000. See [Hornik–Grün](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4180038/) for the underlying axial special-function law, not a priority claim for this trial application.

## Failure test for extending this to a full shell response

If an inner-shell link e lies inside B away from ∂B, then \(E[\mathrm{Var}(h_A\mid\text{all links except }e)]\le Q_A-Q_B\) by the ordering of conditional projections. One can take a maximum or average of such bounds, not their unweighted sum. For a general \(h_A\), a nonlinear remainder can also cancel its linear component's pairing with the generator images: the linear result does not give a bound on \(h_A\) by default. In fact, for any fixed t>0 the degree-three radial polynomial orthogonal to \(1,x,x^2\) has positive conditional variance and is orthogonal to **every** linear generator image (use transverse parity). Thus the restriction to linear responses cannot simply be dropped, even on the genuine SU(2) conditional fiber. The trace boundary profile's projections, a controlled nonlinear remainder, and a block-scale loss still need estimates in the actual joint Wilson equilibrium. No conclusion about continuum decay, a trace-sector gap, or the full mass gap.

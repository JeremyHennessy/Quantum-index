## The vacuum-free Laplace test

For a finite positive-time OS word combination A with a finite-norm vector \(\xi_{i,A}\) in a component i, let
\[
 C_i(A,t)=\langle \xi_{i,A},e^{-tH_i}\xi_{i,A}\rangle
\]
be its **uncentered**, reflection-positive autocorrelation. Fix \(\Delta>0\) in physical time and put
\[
 D_i(A,t)=C_i(A,t)-C_i(A,t+\Delta)
  =\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-tE}(1-e^{-\Delta E})\,d\rho_{i,A}(E).
\]
This difference is nonnegative and automatically removes the full zero-energy sector in each phase, even if their means differ. The multiplier is strictly positive at every positive energy. Its ranges on a total set span densely in the vacuum complement because \(I-e^{-\Delta H_i}\) has kernel exactly the zero-energy sector.

For allowed tests with the mixture identity, \(D_{\rm mix}=\int D_i\,d\mu(i)\) is the identical two-time difference of **uncentered mixed Wilson-limit moments**. If for each such A one can prove
\[
 D_{\rm mix}(A,n\Delta)\le C_A e^{-m n\Delta}\quad
 \text{for every sufficiently large integer }n,
\]
with one common \(m>0\), then the integrated positive measure
\[
 d\sigma_A(E)=\int (1-e^{-\Delta E})\,d\rho_{i,A}(E)\,d\mu(i)
\]
has no mass in \((0,m)\). For any \(b<m\), positive mass on \((0,b]\) would give a lower bound proportional to \(e^{-b n\Delta}\). Nonnegativity then gives \(\rho_{i,A}((0,m))=0\) for almost every phase. Here measurability can be read from the positive correlations on integer times: the pushforward of the finite measure by \(E\mapsto e^{-\Delta E}\) is determined by its moments on [0,1], so integrals of continuous functions are measurable as uniform polynomial limits, and Borel interval masses follow by monotone approximation. The same reasoning justifies integration of the positive spectral measures. No separate lattice insertion for \(P_{\ker H_i}\) or central weights is needed.

For a separated point-word Gram family for which finite-norm component vectors and the time-semigroup pairing are verified, upgrade each A to **every** phase as follows. The function \(D_i(A,\Delta)-e^{-m\Delta}D_i(A,0)\) is continuous in i by the pinned point-kernel statement (only finitely many reflected correlations are involved), and is nonpositive almost everywhere by the preceding support exclusion. Full support gives nonpositivity everywhere. In any one phase this one-time form for all linear combinations A in a dense positive-time family is the Gram certificate for the transformed vectors \((I-e^{-\Delta H_i})^{1/2}\xi_{i,A}\). Thus it implies the selected phase gap provided that the chosen tested family is total. For directly smeared tests instead of point words, continuity through integration must be verified (e.g. a uniform bound on their compact separated supports). These are explicit adapter obligations; this note does not use smoothness in x and pointwise phase continuity to silently assert joint uniform bounds. ## A useful necessary-and-sufficient asymptotic check

For one A with nonzero \(D_{\rm mix}(A,0)\) let \(d_n=D_{\rm mix}(A,n\Delta)\). Under the positive measure just used, \(d_n=\int q^n\,d\tilde\sigma_A(q)\) for \(q=e^{-\Delta E}\in[0,1]\). The ratios \(d_{n+1}/d_n\) are nondecreasing by Cauchy–Schwarz. Their limit is the supremal support q: \(d_n^{1/n}\) tends to that value by restricting the integral to a positive-measure interval within any \(\varepsilon\) of the upper edge, and the same limit follows from the monotone ratios and Cesàro averages of their logarithms. Thus \(-\log(d_{n+1}/d_n)/\Delta\) at finite n is an **upper** bound on the true minimum excitation energy visible to this test, not a lower bound or evidence for a uniform gap. The tilted measure gives negligible weight to very small E but still detects any actual positive mass there in the infinite-time limit. A component with constant expectation only is canceled exactly; a component of arbitrarily small positive weight with slower decay eventually dominates the ratios. These are exact two-atom falsification controls for finite-time curve fitting and vacuum contamination.

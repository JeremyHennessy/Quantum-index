# A bulk limit for local vacuum wavefunction ratios without a global Haar density

**2026-11-25 | Fixed spatial lattice, continuous time | Research only.**

The exact [factorization-preserving band frame](2026-11-25-factorized-band-sign-window.md) removes a spurious mixing of disconnected pieces, but the original quotient still divides pointwise by a vacuum-scale function. An extensive lower bound on that function is not available. A local substitute **is** available. With the same weak real spatial coupling \(|\gamma|\le1/50000\), let \(\Psi_\Lambda>0\) be the real vacuum in an open box. Let fields x,y differ only on a fixed finite set S of links. Then
\[
 \left|\log \frac{\Psi_\Lambda(x)}{\Psi_\Lambda(y)}\right|
 \le C|\gamma|\,|S|.
\]
The log ratio has an open-box bulk limit, uniformly over all fields that agree off S. If two pairs of fields agree on an r-neighborhood of S (with each pair differing only on S), their limiting log ratios differ by at most \(C_S4^{-r+O(1)}\), where r is measured in the spatial co-plaquette link graph. Likewise the error from cutting a box at distance r is exponentially small. This controls a local change in the boundary normalization, not a global infinite-volume vacuum density.

## Local change as an expectation in the exact normalized slab

Write \(Z_{\Lambda,T}(x)=e^{TH}1(x)\), with H and Feynman–Kac paths as in the [free-boundary bulk construction](2026-11-25-free-boundary-bulk-limit.md). For T≥16 use initial segments of length 8, and a last segment of length in [8,16). Use the same product reference space of skeleton endpoints and independent bridge seeds for x and y. Changing the fixed initial field on S changes only the first-segment heat factors on S and the first-segment plaquette factors that touch S. The corresponding change of the unnormalized log density is a bounded function \(J_S\) of the first free slab variables within a single co-plaquette step of S. Its sup norm is at most \(C |S|(10^{-4}+|\gamma|)\), by the one-link log heat estimate and the interaction bound. All its remaining dependence on the fixed fields is local to the same neighborhood. Hence
\[
 \log[Z_{\Lambda,T}(y)/Z_{\Lambda,T}(x)]
   =\log\mathbb E_{\Lambda,T,x}e^{J_S},
\]
where the expectation uses the x-started normalized path measure.

The influence majorant \(C_{ij}=8u_{ij}\) in that construction can be taken symmetric: \(u_{ij}\) is the sum of sup norms over factors containing both i,j. Both row and column sums are below 1/4 even when one box is padded by independent edge paths. Apply [Dobrushin comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117) to the bounded first-slab observable \(e^{J_S}\), and use \(e^{-\|J_S\|_\infty}\) as the uniform lower bound on its expectations when taking logs. Changes in fixed exterior values far from S or deletion of spatial interactions outside the comparison neighborhood affect only conditional kernels at distance at least r minus a fixed collar. The influence-path bound is exponential in r with constants independent of T and volume. This gives spatial locality and the uniform spatial bulk limit of the finite-T log ratio.

For the time limit compare two sufficiently long durations on their common initial segments of length exactly 8. Embed the shorter specification into the longer product of variables, keeping any extra variables as independent references, and compare after the last *common full* initial segment. Interactions or heat factors that differ, including those adjacent to the first noncommon segment, change only kernels a bounded collar from this temporal cut. Equivalently a terminal segment of different length is represented by the same type of seed and endpoint variables and its kernels are allowed discrepancy 1. The observable \(e^{J_S}\) is identical in the common first slab. Each influence step changes the layer index by at most one. Therefore the difference of its expectations, and of their logs, is bounded by \(C_S4^{-\lfloor\min(T,T')/8\rfloor+O(1)}\), uniformly in the spatial box and exterior fields. For each fixed box, positivity, compact spectral resolution and a fixed heat smoothing step identify the limit with \(\log[\Psi_\Lambda(y)/\Psi_\Lambda(x)]\), since the factor \(\langle\Psi_\Lambda,1\rangle\exp(T\lambda_0)\) cancels. The uniform comparison bounds pass to these ratios and exchange the spatial and temporal limits for this local quantity.

## Why the size bound is proportional to the coupling

The raw comparison of weights included a heat term, so it does not itself give O(|γ|). Differentiate \(\log Z_{\Lambda,T}(y)-\log Z_{\Lambda,T}(x)\) in real γ before taking limits. Its derivative is the difference of the sums of normalized expectations of the segment integrals \(B_{p,i}=\int_{\rm segment}\tau_p\,dt\), each bounded by 16. The functions \(B_{p,1}\) touching S may differ explicitly between the two specifications; there are at most four per changed link, for a direct O(|S|) difference. For all other factor observables the functions coincide; their expectation differences are bounded by the comparisons \(C^k b\), with b supported on at most \(C|S|\) discrepant first-slab variables. Each variable is in only a bounded number of segment observables, so after summing all of them, the derivative difference is bounded by a constant times \(\sum_i\sum_{k\ge0}(C^kb)_i\). The **column** bound yields \(\sum_i(C^kb)_i\le (1/4)^k\sum_i b_i\); a row bound alone would not justify this summation. Thus the derivative difference is O(|S|), uniformly in T and volume. At γ=0 both partition functions are exactly one since the heat processes are probability laws. Integrate from γ=0 before taking limits to obtain the asserted \(C|\gamma||S|\) bound.

At zero coupling the result gives ratio one, despite the nonconstant finite first heat factor that would enter a naive pointwise weight comparison. If two disconnected components are present, changing S in one leaves the other component's log weight exactly canceled. The proof does not produce a globally normalizable Haar density or a bound on \(\Psi_\Lambda(x)^{-1}\) alone. The next test is whether a relative estimate for boundary spectral overlaps can be built from these local wavefunction ratios and an appropriately local operator expansion. No sign theorem at arbitrary fixed γ and no spatial continuum claim is implied.

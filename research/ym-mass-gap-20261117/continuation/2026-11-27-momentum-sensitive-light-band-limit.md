# Momentum-sensitive light-band error and a strong-electric scaling obstruction

**2026-11-27 UTC.** Consider the continuous-time SU(2) Hamiltonian H(g)=A−gΣ_p W_p of the preceding notes, on periodic cubic lattices of even side length L≥6. A=(1/2)Σ_e(−Δ_e), W_p=Tr(U_p)/2. Let E(k,g) be the lowest of the three light-band energies in momentum sector k, measured above the interacting vacuum. For sufficiently small nonzero |g| and |k|,

    E(k,g)−E(0,g) = (g²/126)|k|² + O(g²|k|⁴ + g⁴|k|²).

Both constants and the neighborhoods are independent of L. This is a momentum-sensitive real-error estimate, not merely a formal coefficient or a uniform O(g⁴) estimate at each momentum.

## Transport the entire light cluster before compressing

The original-metric comparison of [b69653b](https://github.com/JeremyHennessy/Quantum-index/commit/b69653b0dc1dc219c1e36c66d379670b21ecf8af) holds on the ambient tensor space, including nonphysical states. Take |g| small enough that its relative form error is <1/60 throughout the path from 0 to g. The ambient A-spectrum is contained in the half-integers. Ordered eigenvalues of the vacuum-subtracted interacting operator are squeezed between (1±1/60) times those of A, counted with multiplicities. Hence the whole ambient energy-6 cluster remains separated from the lower and upper clusters by more than 1/4 (compare 5.5 to 6 and 6 to 6.5). There is no need to separate the physical plaquettes from ambient states at the same energy. Choose w with Fourier support in (−1/100,1/100), below both the ambient vacuum gap and all energy-6 cluster-to-complement differences. The same spectral-flow generator intertwines the vacuum projection and the ambient cluster projection. In the instantaneous energy basis, for off-block entries the filter is i/(E_a−E_b); the derivative of each projection is therefore i[D_s,P_s], including when internal levels cross. The unitary commutes with the gauge action, so its restriction transports the physical plaquette band as an exact projection. The compressed dressed operator on that band gives the exact light energies without a momentum-dependent Schur correction. It commutes with translations and spatial inversion by equivariance. See [NSY I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02428) for the spectral-flow and locality framework.

## Weighted remainder and inversion

Apply the finite Duhamel and spectral-flow iteration from [7761fe8](https://github.com/JeremyHennessy/Quantum-index/commit/7761fe8046f8ae5834a42c591a2339147e1b1a2a) with the narrower fixed filter: three magnetic insertions in the raw filtered dynamics cost a bounded local precursor and a cubic time moment; three generator commutators in the parameter expansion cost a fixed number of polynomial support and overlap factors. The filters have every fixed time moment, while locality of the parameter flow propagates every fixed polynomial moment with a finite loss. The fourth-order remainder therefore has original-distance shells bounded by C_N |g|⁴(1+r)^(-N) for arbitrary fixed N. Its vacuum action is scalar. The first-excited-link lifting and telescoping in b69653b give its centered operator as Σ_Y K_Y with K_Y self-adjoint and locally vacuum-annihilating. Moreover, for each fixed m,

    sup_e Σ_(Y containing e) (1+diam Y)^m ||K_Y|| ≤ C_m |g|⁴.

Indeed the lifting/output radii are at most 2r for an input radius r; the telescoping at radius j uses the tail r>j/2. Inserting the diameter weight in the two incidence sums of b69653b costs at most (1+r)^m or (1+j)^m. Taking N>m+10 gives the stated bound. On even tori the center flip of 7761fe8 sends g to −g and is scalar (−I) on the plaquette span, so the compressed odd coefficients vanish.

For normalized plaquette states |p⟩, |q⟩, a term K_Y gives zero matrix entry unless Y meets both supports: it kills the vacuum on Y and self-adjointness allows applying this on either side. For each Y meeting p, the possible q lie in a ball about p of radius O(1+diam Y), so there are O((1+diam Y)^3) of them. Their periodic center displacements have squared length at most C(1+diam Y)^2. The m=5 incidence bound, summed over the four links of p, controls the absolute second spatial moment in every row by C|g|⁴. Although the local decomposition may choose an arbitrary ordering, the summed matrix is translation- and inversion-equivariant. In plaquette-center Bloch coordinates inversion has no orientation-dependent phase; SU(2) fundamental traces are unchanged on reversing the loop. Choose displacements of minimal periodic length, averaging opposite shortest representatives where needed. At the allowed discrete momenta this does not change phases. Thus cosines pair opposite displacements, and |cos(k·x)−1|≤|k|²|x|²/2 proves ||R(k)−R(0)||≤C|g|⁴|k|²; the zeroth moment also gives ||R(0)||≤C|g|⁴.

## Leading symbol and simple eigenvalue

The second-order block is the vacuum-subtracted reduced-resolvent matrix in 7761fe8. For distinct endpoints p,q, parity requires the insertions to be p,q in one of the two orders. Four distinct elementary squares cannot have zero boundary sum: each of the four edges of one face would require a different other face (two elementary faces share at most one edge). Vacuum propagation gives +1/24. For disjoint supports the other intermediate state has energy 12 and gives −1/24. For shared-edge supports, the spin-0 shared-link projection of the product of the two normalized traces is half of the six-link trace, by the fundamental Haar convolution. Since the full product has squared norm one (integrate one unshared link on each face), the other channel has weight 3/4; tensoring two fundamentals leaves only spin 0 and spin 1 on the shared link. Including the factor W_p=Tr(U_p)/2 gives weights 1/16 and 3/16, at energies 9 and 13. The total is 1/24−1/48−3/112=−1/168. The common diagonal is momentum independent by translation and cubic symmetry.

Thus after subtracting a scalar the quadratic block is −J(k)/168, with J the edge adjacency symbol. For orientation ij its diagonal is 2(cos k_i+cos k_j); between ij and il its entry is 4 cos(k_j/2) cos(k_l/2). At zero its top eigenvalue 12 is simple and separated by 12 from the other two; its normalized vector is (1,1,1)/√3. The quadratic expectation of its increment on this vector is −(4/3)|k|². The matrix increment is O(|k|²), so its contribution beyond first-order simple-eigenvalue perturbation is O(|k|⁴). Independent direct edge-set enumeration on L=8 checked 4,608 entries (512 momenta and nine entries each) against the centered symbol including wrap-around faces.

To compare exact and leading dispersion, divide the Bloch matrices after scalar subtraction by g². Join the respective 0 and k matrices by straight segments. In a fixed neighborhood both bottom eigenvalues remain simple with a uniform positive divided gap. Along the segments their eigenprojections differ by O(g²), by a fixed contour resolvent comparison (or a local spectral projection Lipschitz estimate), and the increments differ by O(g²|k|²). Integrating the simple eigenvalue derivatives gives a divided dispersion error O(g²|k|²). This comparison does not differentiate a discrete lattice momentum.

## What spatial refinement does and does not give here

Fix a physical periodic box, write a=box side/L and k=aK for an allowed fixed physical momentum K. For any nonzero g(a) tending to zero,

    [E(aK,g(a))−E(0,g(a))]/[a²g(a)²] → |K|²/126.

No relative rate g=o(a) is required. This is a rest-subtracted nonrelativistic one-band limit only. In fact, the ratio of that band's unscaled dispersion increment to its vacuum gap tends to zero (E(0,g)=6+O(g²)). No common multiplicative rescaling of energy can keep both a finite nonzero gap and a finite nonzero dispersion at fixed nonzero K along this trajectory. The strong-electric path therefore cannot by itself provide the relativistic spatial-continuum target. A new estimate along the matched weak bare-coupling trajectory is necessary; do not extrapolate this expansion into that regime.

[Münster's 1985 paper](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1985/desy85-007.pdf) already constructs an effective isotropic Euclidean Wilson transfer matrix and calculates glueball dispersion through eight nontrivial strong-coupling orders. [Kimura and Ukawa](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(82)90081-5) also computed scalar dispersion in SU(2). Here the point checked is the mixed momentum/coupling/volume error for the continuous-time Hamiltonian in this normalization, not priority for glueball dispersion or for a strong-coupling calculation. No claim of literature novelty, external review, Lean verification, or a Clay solution.

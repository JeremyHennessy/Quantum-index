# Odd-mode signal persists in the continuous-time four-square Hamiltonian

2026-11-27. This removes the time discretization **at fixed spatial lattice**. It does not take spatial volume or lattice spacing to a continuum limit, and it does not solve the 4D mass gap.

Use the straight open four-face model and F=W1 W3-W2 W4 from the [finite-time certificate](../four-square-skipped-neighbor-probe/README.md). Let K_beta be the vacuum-normalized product Wilson convolution, V=sum_i W_i, M=exp(gV/(2 beta)), and T_beta=M K_beta M. Then H_beta=beta(I-T_beta) converges in norm resolvent to H=H0-gV, where H0=1/2 sum_e n_e(n_e+2) in the physical spin-network basis. Here n_e denotes twice spin, with the uncollapsed multiplicity of each spatial edge. At g=.01 the unit-state vacuum-to-odd-level overlap of F in H is between **1.40e-4 and 1.53e-4**. The corresponding transfer-matrix overlap at spatial coupling g/beta converges to this nonzero value (with phases fixed or in modulus).

## Operator limit and identifiable bands

For each fixed label the [large-argument Bessel expansion](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40) gives I_(n+1)(beta)/I_1(beta)=1-n(n+2)/(2 beta)+O_n(beta^-2). For uniform high-label control, the standard integral for integer I_m implies I_(m+1)/I_m is the tilted mean of x in (-1,1) against exp(beta x)(1-x^2)^(m-1/2), by differentiation and the adjacent recurrence. This is in (0,1), and hence the normalized edge eigenvalue decreases with n. If max_e n_e>=N, beta(I-K_beta) is at least beta(1-I_(N+1)/I_1), which tends to N(N+2)/2. Thus diagonal free resolvents converge in norm: first bound the two tails by choosing N, then use pointwise convergence on the finite remaining labels.

The bounded interaction in H_beta is -g(VK_beta+K_beta V)/2+O_g(beta^-1) in operator norm, from the expansion of M. It converges strongly to -gV because K_beta converges strongly to I, and it is uniformly bounded. The limiting free resolvent is compact. Multiplying the difference of these bounded interactions on the right by that compact resolvent gives norm convergence. The resolvent formula at a sufficiently negative real point (uniformly beyond both interaction bounds) now gives the claimed norm-resolvent convergence. Reflection reduces both operators. For each isolated rank-one state below, Riesz projections converge in norm inside the respective sector; bounded F then gives convergence of the overlap.

In the [physical spin-network basis](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9906036) the sum C of length-weighted n(n+2) is at least three times the length-weighted n. The exact enumeration through weight 10 proves C=30 has exactly three binary labels 1234, 124, and 134. One combination is odd; the other two are even. All exterior energies are at least 1 away from 15 and the free vacuum gap is 6. Therefore the vacuum and the energy-15 odd state are each isolated in their respective sectors; bounded perturbation at g=.01 with norm <=.04 preserves these simple levels. The full vacuum remains the unique lowest state.

## Nonzero overlap, including a controlled remainder

In the generalized binary loop convention of the preceding notes choose the odd reference (124-134)/sqrt(2). The linear coefficient is exactly sqrt(2)/96. For W1 W3 the only contributing target is 134. The spatial insertion W4 can be on either side. Both matrix-element products are 1/16 by the binary rule, and their reduced energy denominators are 6 and 12-15=-3, so their sum is -1/96 before the odd state's target phase. Reflection then doubles the phase-weighted contribution for F.

The interval replay uses the same exact signed recoupling matrices and seven-hit envelope as the finite-time fifth-order calculation. Replace Bessel ratios by exact energies C/2. With reference projection normalized to 1, set E_n=-<u0,V u_(n-1)> and u_n=(H0-E0)^-1 Q[V u_(n-1)+sum_(j=1..n-1) E_j u_(n-j)]. The irrelevant degenerate even states are excluded in the odd sector. Through degree five, every direct word and every counterterm is covered by twice-spin <=3 and weight <=18, as in the finite-time replay. Opposite reflected coefficients are checked independently. Even orders vanish by the simultaneous center flip.

On the complex disk |g|<=.075 an energy circle of radius 1/2 isolates the analytic state in each sector, since ||gV||<=.3. Throughout that energy disk the corresponding free complementary inverse has norm <=2. The reference-normalized vector then has perpendicular norm <= alpha/(1-alpha), alpha=8|g|, by the complementary equation. Thus the bilinear transition is bounded by 2(1+(alpha/(1-alpha))^2)<7 on |g|=.075. Its odd Cauchy tail after degree five is bounded by 7(g/.075)^7/(1-(g/.075)^2) at g=.01. Replaying both norms and converting to unit states yields the reported interval. This certificate identifies a limiting signal but gives no explicit finite beta threshold for convergence.

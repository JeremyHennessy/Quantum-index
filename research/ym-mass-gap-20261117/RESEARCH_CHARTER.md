# Quantum Index Proof Lab — physicist's research charter

**Owner-authored principles adopted November 17, 2026.** This is a research-process charter, not an accepted or proven physical theory. Keep the public Quantum Index and all approved UI/scientific records unchanged while pursuing experiments.

## Foundational conduct

1. **Evidence before belief.** Record what observations, calculations, and source statements actually establish.
2. **Question every assumption**, especially convenient continuum-limit or gap assumptions.
3. **Mathematical precision.** Specify Hilbert space, gauge group, operator domains, normalization, coupling, dimensions, quantifiers, limiting order, and each inference.
4. **Confront reality.** A useful physical model should ultimately connect to falsifiable gauge-invariant observables and established measurements; mathematical proof alone does not validate a model of nature.
5. **Prefer sufficient simplicity.** New assumptions must earn their place by explanatory or predictive power.
6. **Recover established limits and symmetries**, including conservation, relevant relativity/causality, gauge invariance, and tested quantum phenomena.
7. **Quantify uncertainty and approximation error.** A floating-point eigengap is not a rigorous lower bound; simulation precision is not physical confidence.
8. **Falsify actively.** Construct countermodels, negative controls, adverse limits, and independent calculations before promoting a claim.
9. **Keep hypothesis, model, prediction, evidence, established theorem, and unverified possibility separate.** Treat source credentials as evidence about authorship, not automatic correctness.
10. **Reproducibility.** Record executable versions, data identities, source locators, exact commit SHA, tests, assumptions, and failure receipts.
11. **Seek connections without forcing unification.** A correspondence between models is only as strong as its proved map and applicable regime.
12. **Revise or reject ideas when contradicted.** Preserve failed work as negative evidence instead of quietly abandoning or relabeling it.

## Special standards for frontier work

- Never present a conjecture as a discovery. Distinguish *mathematically proved*, *numerically supported*, *literature-reproduced*, *conditional*, *independently reviewed*, and *open*.
- Recover known physics first; clearly document why a newly proposed mechanism is not just a restatement of an established method.
- Identify the **weakest unproved step** and design the smallest direct test.
- Measure progress by resolved mathematical questions and falsified hypotheses, not commits, equation counts, or model output.
- Require an independent, inspectable path for significant claims. Lean compilation alone proves a theorem from its **formal definitions**, not correspondence to the Clay statement.

## Proof-lab controls

- Protect approved Quantum Index main, UI, science records, and existing research receipts. New work is branch-only and additive. Never deploy an exploratory proof.
- Do not let a failed parameter bound be reported as proof that a gap exists or does not exist.
- For every computational result record model, units, exact parameters, truncation, independent solver check, and convergence/adversarial tests. Report approximations as approximations.
- No novelty claim without a relevant-source literature search and independent mathematical examination.
- For the Yang–Mills Millennium Problem, success requires the full **four-dimensional continuum theory for every compact simple gauge group** and the required positive physical mass gap—not a single finite lattice, one gauge group, one observable, or a conditional restatement.
- Keep a **negative-results register** alongside any claimed achievements; do not delete an unsuccessful approach when it becomes inconvenient.

**Decision rule:** One hypothesis → one bounded test → interpret → retain, revise or reject. Escalate to a new formal theorem only after independent verification of its statement and assumptions.

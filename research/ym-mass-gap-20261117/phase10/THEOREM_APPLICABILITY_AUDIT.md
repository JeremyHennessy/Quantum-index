## Independent column-site reduction: a more direct chain structure

For a finite connected open two-row N-square ladder, index original links as top horizontals T_i, bottom horizontals B_i (i=0,...,N−1), verticals V_i (i=0,...,N). On the full **unconstrained** Hilbert product
  K_N=⊗_(3N+1 links) L²(SU(2), normalized Haar),
form N+1 *column sites*: site i=0,...,N−1 consists of exactly (T_i,B_i,V_i), and site N consists of V_N alone. This is an isometric rebracketing of tensor factors, **not** a product decomposition of the Gauss-constrained physical Hilbert or a change to the gauge theory. Every face i uses exactly the four original links {T_i,B_i,V_i,V_(i+1)}, so the fundamental Wilson multiplier W_(i+1) is supported precisely on the two neighbouring sites (i,i+1).

Let δ=3α/4. Each site has nonnegative self-adjoint unbounded onsite h_i=α∑_(links in site i) C_e, where C_e=j_e(j_e+1), with a unique constant product vacuum and smallest onsite nonzero eigenvalue δ (Peter–Weyl spin j=1/2). The final one-link site also has the same gap. For α>0 rescale H_N(z)/δ:
  ∑_(i=0)^N (h_i/δ) + τ ∑_(i=0)^(N−1) V_(i,i+1),  τ=z/δ,  V_(i,i+1)=−W_(i+1).
The bounded self-adjoint (for real z) interaction has operator norm ≤2; its quadratic-form weighted norm relative to the two onsite terms is ≤2, and its support interval has length 1, independent of N. No spin cutoff, physical-gap assumption or boundary translation-invariance is introduced.

Since H_N(z) commutes with every original-vertex SU(2) gauge transformation, the full-link analytic isolated **one-dimensional** eigenbranch at z=0, if maintained, carries a continuous one-dimensional representation of the connected semisimple group SU(2)^(2N+2), necessarily the trivial representation. Its eigenvector and Riesz projection belong to/reduce the physical gauge-invariant space. Restriction cannot create a spectrum inside the full-link operator's resolvent set, because the gauge-invariant subspace is reducing for both H_N and H_N* (vertex actions commute also with adjoint H_N(conjugate z)). The physical branch inherits any established eigenvalue separation.

**Scope/correspondence:** The above column-site reduction matches precisely N+1 separable sites, nondegenerate product vacuum, uniformly normalized onsite spectral gap ≥1 and bounded range-1 pair interactions. The original-link SU(2) gauge law is retained through the invariant restriction rather than assumed as a tensor-product constraint. Thus the published theorem implies: for fixed α>0, some **unspecified** z_*(α)>0 independent of N exists on which the full and physical finite-volume Hamiltonians admit an analytic rank-one isolated vacuum-origin eigenbranch E_N(z) and an N-independent **spectral separation** after scaling by δ. The source is an established result, not this lab's new complex-eigenbranch theorem. The proof relies on local Lie–Schwinger transformations and uniform weighted operator estimates.

**Precise limit:** The theorem's "gap" for complex τ is a **spectral separation**, not a positive real-axis spectral gap for a non-selfadjoint operator. A bounded invertible similarity U_N(τ) exists for each N, but the cited result does **not explicitly state the necessary N-independent bound on** ||U_N||·||U_N^(−1)||, global Riesz projection norm, or the trace of the *dynamically centered* observable with the shifted **global** inverse at an arbitrary fixed s>0. Lemma 3.2 bounds a local block inverse on |spectral parameter|≤1/2 during the transformation: it is not the required global sandwiched inverse for *all* Laplace shifts s. Consequently, the original Phase 9 **uniform bound on F_N(s,z)** and its absolute Taylor-coefficient tail remain OPEN. Theorem 3.6 significantly narrows Phase 9's uncertainty about a common **complex eigenbranch disk**, but does not close the full response lemma.

**The one tested inference:** "Volume-uniform complex eigenvalue spectral separation suffices to bound global Riesz projection/operator-resolvent norms uniformly in volume." **False in general.** The exact nonnormal countermodel in ONE_DECISIVE_LEMMA_OR_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md and test_phase10_one_route.py exhibits a separated analytic ground branch with exponentially growing projection norms. ## Other methods — explicitly not silently transferred

- **Kato** isolated-eigenvalue analytic perturbation: at each finite N, H_N(z)=αH_E−zΣW is a type-A entire operator family on the fixed electric domain, but ||ΣW||≤2N gives only a crude N-dependent sufficient radius. The complex Lie–Schwinger theorem is the stronger *published* statement for the eigenbranch.
- **Quantum linked-cluster/polymer expansions**: Yarotsky 2006 §2 derives absolutely convergent space–time polymers for equal-time local insertions under small real couplings; extending these weights to two separated imaginary-time insertions and an integral over unbounded t with a complex-valued coupling is a separate mathematical step. No bound on its weighted connected-polymer sum is quoted without checking it.
- **Lieb–Robinson / complex-time locality**: estimates typically control propagation of local bounded observables under additional interaction assumptions. A real-time commutator bound or finite-time complex evolution is not automatically a uniform Laplace-time ground-state-resolvent bound. See https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-022-04573-w (*Locality Estimates for Complex Time Evolution in 1D*); its low-temperature/observable hypotheses need independent alignment and it is not promoted as proving F_N.
- **Osterwalder–Seiler (1978)** is a Euclidean Wilson lattice theorem, https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8 . Its exact transfer matrix to the continuous-time (2+1)D SU(2) centered resolvent has not been established; journal abstract alone is insufficient.
- **Combes–Thomas/resolvent weighted bounds** may require a spectral or resolvent separation; a non-selfadjoint spectral-distance estimate alone does not bound inverse norms. The exact countermodel is an adverse control.
